<p>760K likes, down to 50K. In less than two months.</p>
<p>AI talent show "12-Star Training" has 539K followers on Douyin. Its debut stage performance video hit 765K likes, the trailer got 734K - when it first launched, it was huge. AI idols, 12 zodiac personas, talent competition format - every angle hit the algorithm jackpot.</p>
<p>But the heat faded fast. By the August Summer Games series of videos, likes were only 75K and 53K - <strong>less than a tenth of the peak</strong>.</p>
<p>From 760K to 50K, behind this drop lies a truth many don't want to face: <strong>AI can replicate every form, but it can't replicate a soul</strong>.</p>

<h2>Why Do Talent Shows Need Real People Anyway?</h2>
<p>Many people might think, talent shows are just singing and dancing performances, right? AI idols sing better, dance tighter, look more perfect - why aren't audiences buying it?</p>
<p>Because the core appeal of talent shows was never the performances themselves.</p>
<p>What truly hooks people about talent shows is <strong>"the sense of growth"</strong> - watching an ordinary person transform from nobody to star, seeing every step from awkward to skilled, from nervous to confident, from insecure to self-assured. You vote, you cheer, you stay up late campaigning - essentially, you're participating in someone's growth.</p>
<p>Then there's <strong>"the sense of reality"</strong> - real people make mistakes, forget lyrics, cry, laugh, freeze up on camera. These "imperfections" are precisely what move people the most. AI idols never make mistakes, they're always perfect - but perfection past a certain point just feels fake.</p>
<p>And most importantly, <strong>"charisma and personality"</strong> - you like a contestant not usually because they sing the best, but because of their personality, their story, how they talk, how they smile. These things are unique, accumulated over a lifetime of experiences.</p>
<p>AI idols are blank slates. You can give them 12 zodiac archetypes, write every line, design every expression - but these are <strong>"designed personalities,"</strong> not real characters. Audiences find it novel for a few episodes, but after a while they realize: no matter how refined these personas are, fundamentally they're no different from a video game character.</p>
<div class="pull-quote">"Do you genuinely cry over a game character's growth? No. After the novelty wears off, you won't genuinely feel for an AI idol's competition either." - An Entertainment Industry Professional</div>
<p>This is the fundamental reason for "12-Star Training's" heat plunge. It's not that production isn't polished enough, not that technology isn't advanced enough - precisely the opposite. The better the tech, the more perfect the visuals, the more it exposes a core flaw: <strong>no soul</strong>.</p>

<h2>AI Content Shouldn't Survive by Imitating Humans</h2>
<p>But AI talent show heat dropping doesn't mean AI content doesn't work.</p>
<p>The problem is <strong>the wrong direction</strong>. A lot of people making AI content immediately think of imitating human content - AI singing like a human, AI dancing like a human, AI acting like a human, AI talent shows like real talent shows. As if the more human-like, the better.</p>
<p>This is a huge misconception.</p>
<p>AI's advantage was never "being like a human" - it's <strong>"things humans can't do."</strong></p>
<p>For example, you can have AI idols streaming 24/7 nonstop - human idols need sleep and rest, AI doesn't. That's an AI-exclusive advantage.</p>
<p>For example, you can let viewers participate in real-time in shaping the AI idol's persona and storyline - a human idol's personality is fixed, an AI idol's can be co-created by the audience. Also an AI-exclusive advantage.</p>
<p>And then there's low cost, fast production, multi-language, multi-style - all things human content either can't do or costs a fortune to do.</p>
<p>If AI talent shows took a different approach: not "AI version of a real talent show," but <strong>"a talent show only AI could do"</strong> - like viewers voting in real time to change contestants' personas, new weekly challenge themes generated on the fly, even viewers generating their own AI characters to compete - the appeal could be completely different.</p>
<p>Current AI talent shows are essentially using more advanced technology to make cheaper substitutes for real talent shows. Audiences leave after the novelty wears off - that's only normal.</p>
<p>But the real opportunity for AI content isn't in imitating what human content looks like - it's in <strong>exploring forms human content could never achieve</strong>.</p>
<p>That direction is the future of AI content.</p>
