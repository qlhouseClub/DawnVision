10,000-Unit Capacity Hits Liuzhou:
UBTECH's Robots Building Robots Is Real

优必选柳州万台级人形机器人工厂投产，全球首个适配万台级产能的智能制造标杆，广西首个"用机器人造机器人"闭环工厂。

UBTECH's Liuzhou 10,000-unit humanoid robot factory opens — the world's first manufacturing facility scaled for 10K-unit capacity and Guangxi's first closed-loop 'robots building robots' plant.

No.052 2026.09.14 约 5 分钟阅读 ~5 min read

1.4万平方米的厂房里，Walker S系列人形机器人正在组装下一代Walker S系列人形机器人。9月12日，柳州优必选工业人形机器人超级智慧工厂正式投产，广西首个"用机器人造机器人"的闭环制造体系开始运转。这是全球首个适配万台级产能的人形机器人智能制造工厂。

从首台到万台：一年多跑完的产业跳跃

回顾优必选的时间线，速度令人侧目。从首台Walker S原型机突破，到千台级小批量产，再到如今万台级产能的工厂投产，整个过程仅用了一年多。对比汽车行业从原型到量产动辄三到五年的周期，这个速度几乎是压缩了两到三倍。

快速的背后有两层原因。第一，人形机器人的零部件供应链和工业机器人、自动化设备高度重叠，优必选作为中国人形机器人头部公司，供应链积累已经超过十年。第二，柳州的选择并非偶然——广西是中国面向东盟的制造业门户，土地、人工、政策成本都远低于一线城市，而当地完善的汽车产业配套可以直接服务于机器人制造。

"用机器人造机器人"：不只是宣传口号

"用机器人造机器人"听起来像营销话术，但在工程层面它意味着一个关键的能力闭环：你造出的机器人足够可靠，可以胜任精密制造任务；你的产线足够成熟，能容纳自家产品的自动化装配。这两件事同时成立，才是真正的技术门槛。

工厂主要生产Walker S系列和Cruzr系列，前者面向工业场景（巡检、搬运、装配），后者面向服务场景（导览、接待）。两个系列共享大量底层模块化设计，使得同一产线可以灵活切换产品型号。这对万台级产能的工厂至关重要——没有足够的产线灵活性，万台级产能反而会成为库存负担。

规模化量产：具身智能的真正分水岭

如果说小鹏IRON代表了"造车公司跨界造机器人"的路径，那么优必选代表的是另一条路：专业机器人公司的规模化。前者的优势在于汽车制造经验和资金实力，后者的优势在于十年以上的人形机器人技术积累和对行业的深度理解。

两条路径的共同指向是：具身智能正在从实验室走向工厂，从demo走向量产。万台级产能不是终点，而是一个信号——当产能突破万台，人形机器人的单位成本才有可能降到企业客户愿意大规模采购的区间。

柳州工厂的投产，标志着具身智能正式进入规模化量产阶段。但"投产"和"满产"之间还有很长的路要走。订单能否填满产能，良率能否维持高位，海外市场能否打开——这些问题的答案，将在未来12个月内逐步揭晓。

明天见。

Inside a 14,000-square-meter factory, Walker S humanoid robots are assembling the next generation of Walker S humanoid robots. On September 12, UBTECH's Liuzhou Industrial Humanoid Robot Super Smart Factory officially opened — Guangxi's first closed-loop manufacturing system where "robots build robots." It's the world's first smart manufacturing facility designed for 10,000-unit humanoid robot capacity.

From First Unit to Ten Thousand: An Industrial Leap in Just Over a Year

UBTECH's timeline is striking. From the first Walker S prototype breakthrough, to small-batch production of hundreds, to today's 10,000-unit-capacity factory — the entire arc took just over a year. Compared to the three-to-five-year cycle typical for automobiles from prototype to mass production, this pace is compressed by two to three times.

Two factors explain the speed. First, humanoid robot component supply chains heavily overlap with industrial robotics and automation equipment. As China's leading humanoid robot company, UBTECH has over a decade of supply chain accumulation. Second, Liuzhou wasn't a random choice — Guangxi is China's manufacturing gateway to ASEAN, with land, labor, and policy costs far below first-tier cities, while the local automotive industry infrastructure directly serves robot manufacturing.

'Robots Building Robots': More Than a Marketing Line

"Robots building robots" sounds like marketing copy, but at the engineering level it represents a critical capability loop: the robots you build must be reliable enough for precision manufacturing, and your production line must be mature enough to accommodate automated assembly of your own products. Both conditions being true simultaneously is the real technical barrier.

The factory primarily produces Walker S series (for industrial scenarios: inspection, transport, assembly) and Cruzr series (for service scenarios: reception, wayfinding). Both lines share extensive modular base designs, allowing flexible product switching on the same line. This is critical for a 10,000-unit factory — without production line flexibility, that capacity becomes an inventory liability.

Mass Production: Embodied AI's Real Watershed

If XPeng's IRON represents the "carmaker crosses into robotics" path, UBTECH represents the alternative: a specialized robotics company scaling up. The former's advantage lies in automotive manufacturing experience and capital; the latter's lies in over a decade of humanoid robot R&D and deep industry understanding.

Both paths point to the same destination: embodied AI is moving from lab to factory, from demo to mass production. 10,000-unit capacity isn't the finish line — it's the signal that when capacity crosses the 10,000 threshold, humanoid robot unit costs finally enter the range where enterprise customers are willing to buy at scale.

The Liuzhou factory's opening marks embodied AI's official entry into scaled mass production. But between "open" and "full capacity" lies a long road. Whether orders can fill capacity, whether yields stay high, whether overseas markets open — the answers will unfold over the next 12 months.
