Groq 估值腰斩近 50% 再融 3.5 亿 屠龙少年成了恶龙的合作伙伴

Groq Raises at $3.5B, Nearly Halved Dragon-Slayer Becomes the Dragon's Partner

从 69 亿到 35 亿，从自研 LPU 到卖英伟达算力，从敌人到合作伙伴。AI 芯片创业最黑色幽默的故事。

From $6.9B to $3.5B, from homegrown LPU to selling Nvidia compute, from enemy to partner. The blackest comedy in AI chip startups.

No.039 2026年8月20日 约 5 分钟阅读 ~5 min read

今天给大家讲一个非常有戏剧性的故事。故事的主角叫 Groq，曾经是 AI 芯片界最耀眼的独角兽之一，喊着要用自研 LPU 芯片干翻英伟达的 GPU。现在呢？它融了 3.5 亿美元 A 轮，估值 35 亿——听起来挺多对吧？但去年 9 月它的估值还是 69 亿，差不多砍了一半。

更离谱的是，这轮融资的领投方叫 Disruptive，英伟达还计划参投。

是的你没看错。那个曾经要干翻英伟达的公司，现在变成了英伟达的合作伙伴，英伟达还要投资它。这剧情，比爽文还跌宕。

屠龙少年的崛起与坠落

把时间拨回几年前。Groq 由前谷歌 TPU 工程师乔纳森·罗斯在 2016 年创办，主打自研 LPU（语言处理器）芯片，号称 AI 推理延迟比 GPU 低、吞吐量比 GPU 高。那时候的 Groq 是妥妥的明星创业公司——从种子轮到 D 轮，累计拿了 17.5 亿美元融资，投资方名单星光熠熠：Social Capital、老虎环球、D1 Capital、贝莱德、思科、三星……

那时候 Groq 的叙事是什么？"我们要做推理时代的英伟达杀手。"

然后 2025 年 12 月，剧情急转直下。英伟达和 Groq 达成了一笔价值约 200 亿美元的技术授权交易。交易内容是什么呢？英伟达获得 Groq 相关技术的使用权，然后——创始人兼 CEO 乔纳森·罗斯、时任总裁桑尼·马德拉、以及多名核心员工，集体加入了英伟达。

等等，这不是收购啊。Groq 还是一个独立公司。但是核心团队去了英伟达，技术授权给了英伟达，那 Groq 剩下了啥？

剩下一个空壳，和一个需要重新找方向的业务。

从自研芯片到"英伟达算力经销商"

团队走了，技术授权了，Groq 怎么办？转型呗。

从自研 LPU 芯片公司，转型成基于英伟达系统的 AI 推理云运营商。说白了就是——我不造芯片了，我买英伟达的芯片，搭成集群，卖给客户做推理算力。

是不是有点眼熟？对，这不就是很多云厂商干的事嘛。曾经的"英伟达杀手"，现在成了英伟达的客户，还帮英伟达卖算力。

而且这次融资之所以叫 A 轮，就是因为相当于团队、业务、资本结构全部调整之后的新阶段首轮融资。以前的估值体系全部推倒重来。69 亿 → 35 亿，砍了近一半。Groq 说这不是"降价融资"，而是英伟达技术授权交易完成后的"重新估值"。

嗯，你说是就是吧。

更有意思的是，今年 6 月 Groq 已经拿了 6.5 亿美元的过渡性资金，加上这轮 3.5 亿，两个月一共拿了 10 亿美元。说明什么？说明烧钱速度很快。转型推理云是重资产业务——买芯片、建数据中心、运营维护，样样都要花钱。

Groq 现在的日子怎么样

按 Groq 自己的说法，现在还挺不错的。运营 13 座数据中心，服务超过 600 万开发者，平台每周处理数万亿 Token。计划明年把算力规模从 54 兆瓦扩到 200 兆瓦以上，增幅 270%。

但是——营收多少？客户付费率怎么样？数据中心利用率多少？盈不盈利？新增算力里英伟达 GPU 和自研 LPU 各占多少？这些关键数据，Groq 一个都没说。

你品，你细品。

而且英伟达的参与也很微妙——"计划参投"，"尚待完成交割"。也就是说现在还没真金白银投进来。这到底是真投资，还是给 Groq 站台、帮它把故事讲下去？

如果英伟达真的投了，那简直是 AI 圈最黑色幽默的时刻：曾经喊着要颠覆你的对手，最后被你收编成了分销商，你还象征性地投它一笔，告诉全世界——看，连我的敌人，最后都成了我的合作伙伴。

几个扎心的提醒

故事讲完了，说几句掏心窝子的话。

第一，做硬科技创业，小心降维打击。你辛辛苦苦做了好多年的技术，巨头一句话、一笔钱，连人带技术全给你端了。你以为你在和巨头竞争，其实巨头根本没把你当竞争对手——你只是它的人才库和技术储备池。

第二，"干翻巨头"这种叙事，听听就好。大部分喊着要颠覆行业的创业公司，最后最好的结局不是干翻巨头，而是被巨头收购或者收编。Groq 其实算运气好的了——至少团队被高价"收编"了，投资人也通过技术授权交易拿到了回报。更多的芯片创业公司，连这一步都走不到。

第三，AI 算力这门生意，最后拼的不是技术，是资本和规模。你有再厉害的技术，没有几百亿砸数据中心，你就做不了生意。Groq 现在转型推理云，本质上就是从一个技术公司变成了一个重资产运营公司。而重资产这个赛道，巨头的优势是碾压级的。

所以啊，屠龙少年的故事听听就好。现实中，大多数屠龙少年最后要么被龙收编，要么……就没有要么了。

今天就槽到这里，明天继续。

Today we've got a story with real dramatic flair. Its protagonist is Groq — once one of the brightest unicorns in AI chips, loudly proclaiming it would dethrone Nvidia's GPUs with its homegrown LPU silicon. Today? It just raised a $350 million Series A at a $3.5 billion valuation. Sounds like a lot, right? Except last September it was valued at $6.9 billion. Roughly cut in half.

And the kicker? The round is led by Disruptive, and Nvidia is planning to participate.

Yes, you read that correctly. The company that once wanted to destroy Nvidia is now Nvidia's partner, and Nvidia is even investing in it. The plot twists harder than a prestige drama.

Rise and Fall of the Dragon-Slayer

Let's rewind a few years. Groq was founded in 2016 by Jonathan Ross, a former Google TPU engineer, with a bold thesis: build its own LPU (Language Processing Unit) chips that deliver lower latency and higher throughput than GPUs for AI inference. Back then, Groq was the star startup — from seed through Series D, it raised over $1.75 billion from a who's who of investors: Social Capital, Tiger Global, D1 Capital, BlackRock, Cisco, Samsung… the list goes on.

Groq's narrative back then? "We're going to be the Nvidia killer of the inference era."

Then December 2025 happened. The plot took a sharp turn. Nvidia and Groq announced a technology licensing deal worth approximately $20 billion. The details? Nvidia got access to Groq's technology, and — here's the kicker — founder and CEO Jonathan Ross, president Sunny Madra, and multiple core employees all joined Nvidia.

Wait, this wasn't an acquisition. Groq remained an independent company. But the core team left for Nvidia, and the technology was licensed to Nvidia. So what was left of Groq?

An empty shell, and a business that needed a complete reinvention.

From Chip Designer to "Nvidia Compute Reseller"

Team gone, technology licensed out — what does Groq do? Pivot, obviously.

From a homegrown LPU chip company to an AI inference cloud operator running on Nvidia systems. Translation: we don't make chips anymore. We buy Nvidia chips, build clusters, and sell inference compute to customers.

Sound familiar? Yeah — that's basically what cloud providers do. The once-mighty "Nvidia killer" is now an Nvidia customer, helping Nvidia sell compute.

And the reason this round is called "Series A" is because it's effectively the first round of a new phase — new team, new business, new capital structure. The old valuation is gone. $6.9B → $3.5B, nearly halved. Groq says this isn't a "down round" — it's a "re-valuation" after the Nvidia technology licensing deal.

Sure. Whatever you say.

What's even more interesting is that Groq already raised $650 million in bridge financing back in June. Add this $350 million, and that's $1 billion in two months. Translation: the burn rate is high. Pivoting to inference cloud is a capital-intensive business — buying chips, building data centers, operations and maintenance — everything costs money.

How's Groq Doing, Really?

By Groq's own account, things are going pretty well. Thirteen data centers in operation, serving over 6 million developers, processing trillions of tokens per week. Plans to expand compute capacity from 54 MW to over 200 MW next year — a 270% increase.

But — what's the revenue? What's the customer pay rate? What's the data center utilization? Is it profitable? What's the split between Nvidia GPUs and homegrown LPUs in the new capacity? On these key questions, Groq has… nothing to say.

Draw your own conclusions.

And Nvidia's involvement is also a bit cryptic — "plans to invest," "pending closing." Which means the money isn't actually in yet. Is this a real investment, or just站台 (standing on stage) to help Groq keep the story going?

If Nvidia actually does invest, it would be the blackest comedy moment in AI history: the rival that once vowed to overthrow you ends up becoming your distributor, and you throw it a symbolic investment to show the world — look, even my enemies eventually become my partners.

A Few Hard Truths

Story time's over. A few honest takeaways.

First, if you're building hard tech, watch out for dimensional reduction strikes. You spend years building technology, and then a giant shows up and acquires your team and your IP with one deal and one check. You think you're competing with them — but they don't even see you as a competitor. You're just a talent pool and a technology reserve.

Second, "destroying the giant" is a narrative, not a strategy. Most startups that shout about disrupting the industry end up with their best outcome being acquired or absorbed by the very giant they targeted. Groq is actually lucky — at least the team got a high-priced "recruitment," and investors got paid back through the licensing deal. Most chip startups don't even make it that far.

Third, in the AI compute business, what matters in the end isn't technology — it's capital and scale. No matter how impressive your tech is, without billions to throw at data centers, you can't do business. Groq's pivot to inference cloud essentially transforms it from a technology company into a capital-heavy operator. And in heavy assets, the giants have crushing advantages.

So yeah — dragon-slayer stories are fun to listen to. In reality, most dragon-slayers either get recruited by the dragon… or they just don't make it.

That's it for today's roast. More tomorrow.

The wildest part isn't the valuation being cut in half — it's that Nvidia is planning to participate in this round. From enemy to partner, the dragon-slayer becomes the dragon's reseller.
