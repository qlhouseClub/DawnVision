8月3日晚，OpenAI在官方博客发布了一篇题为《Apple is getting this wrong》的文章，正式回应Apple7月底提起的商业秘密诉讼。这不是科技圈常见的"不予置评"或"我们会在法庭上回应"——OpenAI选择了一种极其罕见的方式：直接把证据摆到桌面上。文章不仅逐条反驳了Apple的指控，还附上了员工之间的聊天记录作为佐证，把两家公司从合作走向决裂的过程摊在了公众面前。

距离Apple在加州法院提起诉讼仅仅过去一周。一周前，大多数人还在猜测OpenAI会选择静默应对，毕竟和Apple这样的公司打官司，公开对抗往往不是最优解。但Sam Altman选择了最激进的路线：不仅要应诉，还要在舆论场上先赢一局。这篇博客发出后迅速在科技圈刷屏，登上Hacker News热门榜，在Twitter/X上引发大量讨论。

为什么是公开反驳，不是私下和解

要理解这件事的分量，你得先明白科技公司之间的诉讼通常怎么打。

绝大多数商业秘密诉讼的剧本是这样的：A公司起诉，B公司发表一个不痛不痒的声明说"我们认为指控毫无根据"，然后双方进入漫长的证据开示程序，打个一两年最后要么和解要么撤诉。公众几乎永远看不到完整的证据，所有交锋都在法庭的密封文件里进行。这种打法对大公司最有利——既维护了法务部门的KPI，又不会让任何一方在公众面前太难看。

OpenAI这次完全不按剧本走。文章开头第一句就定调："Apple对我们的指控是错误的，我们想把事实讲清楚。"然后逐条反驳：关于员工跳槽带走商业秘密的指控，OpenAI放出了该员工离职前与Apple经理的聊天记录，记录显示Apple方面明确表示"你不需要带任何东西过来"；关于ChatGPT集成Siri期间获取敏感信息的指控，OpenAI详细列出了双方签署的访问权限协议，证明所有数据访问都在Apple划定的范围内；关于OpenAI"不当使用"Apple技术的说法，OpenAI直接贴出了多封Apple工程师主动发来的技术建议邮件。

"在科技行业，打官司不是为了赢，是为了让对手难受。但OpenAI这次选择公开证据，说明他们要的不止是不难受——他们要的是舆论场的主动权。"—— 硅谷科技法律界人士评论

这招够狠。商业秘密诉讼的核心就是信息不对称——原告可以模糊地声称"商业秘密被窃取"，不需要一开始就拿出具体证据。但OpenAI直接把聊天记录甩出来，就把球踢回了Apple脚下：你说我偷东西，我把对话放出来了，你现在说我哪里偷了？如果Apple后续不能拿出更有力的证据，在舆论场上就已经输了一半。

从蜜月到决裂：Siri集成背后的利益纠葛

要理解这场官司的根源，得回到2025年WWDC。

当时Apple在舞台上宣布与OpenAI达成合作，ChatGPT将深度集成进iOS 18的Siri。Tim Cook和Sam Altman同台微笑，媒体用"世纪合作"来形容这桩交易。Apple拿到了当时最强的大模型能力，终于能在AI时代交差；OpenAI拿到了iPhone十几亿用户的入口，看起来是双赢。

但蜜月期比所有人预期的都短。问题出在两个根本矛盾上。第一是数据控制权。根据合作协议，Siri调用ChatGPT时的用户数据如何使用、如何存储、双方各有什么权限，这些细节从一开始就没完全谈拢。Apple坚持用户数据必须留在Apple这边，OpenAI认为合理的模型改进需要访问交互数据。双方在这个问题上拉扯了数月，集成效果远没有发布会上演示的那么流畅。

第二是产品路线冲突。OpenAI在2026年加速推自己的桌面端应用和Agent生态，这直接触碰到了Apple的底线——Apple不允许在自己的平台上出现一个不受控制的AI入口。当OpenAI发布ChatGPT桌面版的电脑控制功能时，Apple多次以安全为由要求修改，双方矛盾彻底公开化。

026期我们写过Apple起诉OpenAI的新闻，当时的判断是这是"一次战术性施压"，目的是在合作续约谈判中拿到更好的条件。但从OpenAI这次强硬的公开回应来看，事情可能比我们想的更严重——OpenAI似乎已经放弃了与Apple维持表面和平的打算，准备硬刚到底。

AI时代的战争规则变了

这场诉讼最值得注意的不是谁对谁错，而是它预示的战争规则变化。

在PC时代和移动互联网时代，科技巨头之间的战争主要发生在三个战场：产品发布会、应用商店审核、法庭。前两个战场有公关团队和审核规则作为缓冲，第三个战场有保密协议和密封文件作为遮羞布。公众看到的永远是经过精心包装的叙事，真正的杀招都在桌子底下。

但OpenAI这次选择了第四个战场：公开舆论场的证据战。直接把原始聊天记录放出来，让公众自己判断谁对谁错。这一招在AI时代之前很少有人用，因为科技纠纷的细节往往太技术化，公众看不懂也不关心。但AI不一样——ChatGPT和Siri是几亿人每天都在用的产品，这场官司的结果直接关系到用户的数据安全和产品体验，公众有强烈的围观动机。

更重要的是，OpenAI作为一家"以透明为价值观"的AI公司，公开证据这一招与其品牌形象高度契合。当你的对手是一家以保密文化著称的消费电子公司时，"我把所有东西都放出来给大家看"本身就是一种极其有效的公关攻击。你Apple不是说我偷你东西吗？好，我把聊天记录全放出来，你敢放吗？

这可能开创一个先例。未来科技巨头之间的诉讼，越来越多的公司会选择把关键证据公开化。与其让法官在封闭的法庭里判断，不如先让几亿网民当一次陪审团。毕竟在AI时代，公众信任本身就是最核心的商业资产。

真正的输家是谁

官司不管谁赢，有一件事几乎是确定的：Apple和OpenAI的深度合作已经名存实亡。

对Apple来说，这意味着Siri的AI升级之路将更加坎坷。原本依赖ChatGPT补全的能力，现在要么靠自己的模型慢慢赶，要么去找Google或Anthropic谈合作。但Google是Android的东家，Anthropic和Amazon走得更近，没有一个选项是完美的。027期我们写过Siri修复后反响平淡的新闻——如果没有强大的第三方模型支撑，Apple Intelligence的体验差距只会越拉越大。

对OpenAI来说，失去iPhone这个超级入口当然是损失，但远没有到致命的程度。ChatGPT自己的应用已经有几亿月活，桌面端和Agent生态正在快速成型，企业端业务增长迅猛。更重要的是，OpenAI正在证明一件事：没有任何一个平台能卡住我的脖子。这种信号传递给其他平台方（Google、Microsoft、手机厂商）的威慑力，可能比输掉一场官司的代价大得多。

真正值得关注的是这件事对整个AI生态的影响。当两大巨头从合作走向对抗，最受伤的其实是用户——你手机里的Siri和你用的ChatGPT之间，可能再也不会有顺畅的连接了。围墙花园在AI时代不仅没有被推倒，反而越建越高。

但话说回来，这或许就是科技行业的本质。没有永远的朋友，没有永远的敌人，只有永远的利益。昨天还在发布会上微笑握手，今天就在博客上甩证据对质。比起虚伪的和平，或许我们更应该欣赏这种直白的交锋——至少你知道双方在争什么，至少证据摆在那里让你自己判断。

A 2,000-word essay with chat log screenshots — no cease-and-desist warnings, just direct public rebuttal.

On the evening of August 3, OpenAI published an article titled "Apple Is Getting This Wrong" on its official blog, formally responding to the trade secret lawsuit Apple filed in late July. This isn't the tech industry's standard "no comment" or "we will respond in court." OpenAI chose an extraordinarily rare approach: laying the evidence directly on the table. The article not only refutes Apple's claims point by point but also attaches employee chat logs as supporting evidence, laying bare for the public how the two companies went from partnership to breakup.

Only a week had passed since Apple filed suit in California court. A week ago, most observers assumed OpenAI would choose silence — after all, when fighting a company like Apple, public confrontation is rarely the optimal move. The post quickly spread through tech circles after publication, topping Hacker News hot list and driving massive discussion on Twitter/X.

Why Public Rebuttal Instead of Private Settlement

To understand the weight of this, you need to know how tech company lawsuits normally play out.

The standard trade-secret lawsuit script goes like this: Company A sues, Company B issues a bland statement saying "we believe the claims are without merit," then both sides enter a long discovery phase, fight for a year or two, and eventually either settle or drop the case. The public almost never sees complete evidence — every exchange happens under sealed court filings. This approach works best for big companies: it satisfies legal department KPIs without making either side look too bad in public.

OpenAI threw this playbook out the window. The opening line sets the tone: "Apple's claims against us are wrong, and we want to set the record straight." Then point-by-point rebuttal: on the claim that a departing employee took trade secrets, OpenAI releases the employee's chat logs with his Apple manager before departure, showing Apple explicitly saying "you don't need to bring anything over"; on claims that OpenAI accessed sensitive information during the Siri integration, OpenAI details the access agreements both sides signed, proving all data access stayed within Apple-drawn boundaries; on claims of "improper use" of Apple technology, OpenAI posts emails from Apple engineers proactively sending technical suggestions.

"In tech, lawsuits aren't about winning — they're about making the other guy uncomfortable. But OpenAI releasing evidence publicly means they don't just want to avoid discomfort — they want the upper hand in the court of public opinion."— Silicon Valley tech legal observer

It's a ruthlessly effective move. The core of trade secret litigation is information asymmetry — the plaintiff can vaguely claim "trade secrets were stolen" without producing concrete evidence upfront. But by dropping chat logs publicly, OpenAI kicks the ball back to Apple's court: you say I stole something, I'm releasing the conversations — where exactly is the theft? If Apple can't produce stronger evidence, they've already lost half the battle in the court of public opinion.

Honeymoon to Breakup: The Interest Conflicts Behind Siri Integration

To understand the root of this lawsuit, go back to WWDC 2025.

That's when Apple took the stage to announce its partnership with OpenAI — ChatGPT would be deeply integrated into Siri in iOS 18. Tim Cook and Sam Altman smiled side by side, and media called it a "deal of the century." Apple got the strongest LLM capabilities available, finally able to deliver in the AI era; OpenAI got access to a billion-plus iPhone users. It looked like a win-win.

The honeymoon was shorter than anyone expected. Two fundamental conflicts derailed it. First, data control. Under the partnership agreement, details around how user data from Siri-ChatGPT calls would be used, stored, and what permissions each side had were never fully resolved from the start. Apple insisted user data must stay on Apple's side; OpenAI argued reasonable model improvement required access to interaction data. The two sides haggled for months, and the integration experience was never as smooth as the onstage demo suggested.

Second, product roadmap conflicts. When OpenAI accelerated its desktop app and Agent ecosystem push in 2026, it directly touched Apple's bottom line — Apple doesn't allow an uncontrolled AI entry point on its platform. When OpenAI released ChatGPT Desktop's computer control feature, Apple repeatedly demanded changes citing security concerns, and the conflict went fully public.

In Issue 026 we covered Apple's lawsuit against OpenAI, judging it as "tactical pressure" to get better terms in partnership renewal negotiations. But OpenAI's forceful public response suggests things may be more serious than we thought — OpenAI seems to have abandoned maintaining surface-level peace with Apple and is ready for a bare-knuckle fight.

The Rules of War Have Changed in the AI Era

The most notable thing about this lawsuit isn't who's right or wrong — it's the shift in warfare rules it foreshadows.

In the PC era and mobile internet era, tech giant wars played out on three main battlefields: product launches, app store review, and court. The first two had PR teams and review rules as buffers; the third had NDAs and sealed filings as fig leaves. The public always saw carefully packaged narratives, with the real kill shots happening under the table.

But OpenAI chose a fourth battlefield: evidence warfare in the public square. Releasing raw chat logs directly, letting the public judge who's right and wrong. Few companies used this tactic before the AI era, because tech dispute details were usually too technical for the public to understand or care about. But AI is different — ChatGPT and Siri are products used by hundreds of millions daily, and the outcome of this lawsuit directly affects user data security and product experience. The public has strong motivation to watch.

More importantly, as an AI company whose stated values include "transparency," the evidence-release play aligns perfectly with OpenAI's brand. When your opponent is a consumer electronics company famous for its culture of secrecy, "I'm putting everything out there for everyone to see" is itself a devastating PR attack. You say I stole from you, Apple? Fine — I'm releasing all the chat logs. Do you dare do the same?

This may set a precedent. Future tech giant lawsuits will see more companies choosing to make key evidence public. Rather than letting a judge decide in a closed courtroom, why not let hundreds of millions of netizens be the jury first? In the AI era, public trust itself is the core commercial asset.

Who's the Real Loser

Whoever wins the lawsuit, one thing is virtually certain: the deep partnership between Apple and OpenAI is effectively dead.

For Apple, this means Siri's AI upgrade path gets rockier. Capabilities originally plugged by ChatGPT will now have to be built slowly with in-house models, or replaced through new deals with Google or Anthropic. But Google owns Android; Anthropic is closer to Amazon. Neither option is perfect. We covered in Issue 027 how Siri's revamp landed with a thud — without strong third-party model backing, the Apple Intelligence experience gap will only widen.

For OpenAI, losing the iPhone super-entry-point is a loss, but far from fatal. ChatGPT's own app has hundreds of millions of MAUs, desktop and Agent ecosystems are growing fast, and enterprise revenue is surging. More importantly, OpenAI is proving one thing: no single platform can put a chokehold on me. That signal to other platform holders (Google, Microsoft, device makers) carries more deterrent weight than the cost of losing one lawsuit.

What's truly worth watching is the impact on the whole AI ecosystem. When two giants go from partnership to confrontation, the real losers are users — the Siri on your phone and the ChatGPT you use may never connect smoothly again. The walled garden isn't being torn down in the AI era; it's being built higher.

But then again, this might be the nature of tech. No permanent friends, no permanent enemies — only permanent interests. Shaking hands and smiling at a keynote one day, dumping evidence in a blog post the next. Rather than hypocritical peace, maybe we should appreciate this kind of straightforward clash — at least you know what they're fighting over, at least the evidence is out there for you to judge.
