这就是最近抖音上爆火的"人生副本"类短视频。固定的开篇句式、第二人称"你"的叙事视角、AI生成的漫画风格画面、配音演员的旁白——整个内容没有一个真人出镜，却能让数万甚至数十万观众沉浸其中，跟着"你"的人生经历一起哭一起笑。

这类视频的话题播放量已经突破十几亿，一大批博主靠着这个赛道快速涨粉——最快的账号一夜之间涨粉数十万。MCN机构闻风而动，蜂群文化已经搭建起60多个AI账号矩阵，目标年底突破100个。

为什么是"人生副本"火了？

AI短视频不是什么新鲜事了。为什么偏偏是"人生副本"这个品类火了？答案藏在三个关键设计里。

第一个是第二人称叙事。用"你"来讲故事，和用"他/她"来讲故事，沉浸感完全不是一个量级的。传统的故事视频是"看别人的故事"，而人生副本是"体验别人的人生"——你就是主角，你经历了一切。这种代入感是传统叙事方式很难达到的。

第二个是AI原生的视觉风格。不是真人实拍，也不是传统手绘动画，而是AI生成的漫画/插画风格。这种风格有几个好处：第一，成本极低，几分钟就能生成一张高质量画面；第二，风格统一，不会出现真人演员演技参差不齐的问题；第三，因为不是真人，反而更容易让人代入——你不会因为演员长得好不好看而分心，你只会关注故事本身。

第三个是情绪价值拉满。人生副本选题大多聚焦在"普通人的逆袭""失败者的重生""遗憾的弥补"这类主题上——都是人们内心深处最渴望、最有共鸣的东西。现实中你可能过着平凡的生活，但在这两三分钟的视频里，你可以体验一段完全不同的人生。这种"情绪代餐"的价值，在压力越来越大的当下，格外有市场。

所以你看，这不是简单的"用AI做视频"——这是AI原生的内容形态。它的叙事方式、视觉风格、生产流程，全部都是为AI量身定制的。你用传统的制作思路根本做不出这个成本结构，也做不出这个更新速度。

内容行业的降维打击才刚刚开始

"人生副本"的爆火，只是AI内容革命的一个缩影。更大的变化正在发生。

先看成本。根据行业数据，传统3分钟二维动画的平均制作周期是18.5天，单分钟成本约8000元。到了2026年，借助AI工作流，同等画质的3分钟动画制作周期压缩到了3天以内，单分钟成本暴跌至150元。效率提升了6倍，成本下降了98%。这是什么概念？就是原来一个团队一个月做两条片子，现在一个人一个月能做20条。

再看门槛。以前做短视频，你需要会拍摄、会剪辑、会写文案、可能还要会演戏。现在呢？只要你有想法、会写Prompt，就能生成一条还不错的视频。技术门槛崩塌了，创意门槛凸显了。以后内容行业比拼的不再是"谁的制作技术好"，而是"谁的创意更棒、谁更懂用户的情绪"。

但这也带来了一个问题：同质化。当工具变得如此简单，当大家都在用同样的模型生成类似的画面、写类似的文案时，内容的差异化在哪里？答案可能是：人格化、垂类深度、独特视角。就像博客时代人人都能写，但真正跑出来的是那些有独特风格和深度的博主；视频时代人人都能拍，但真正火的是那些有人格魅力的创作者。AI时代也一样——工具让每个人都能做内容，但真正能脱颖而出的，还是那些有独特灵魂的人。

蜂群文化的60个AI账号矩阵，MCN的批量入场，说明AI内容已经从"个人玩家试水"进入了"机构化运作"阶段。接下来的竞争会更激烈，但机会也更多——因为市场在快速膨胀，新的内容形态、新的平台、新的商业模式，都在不断涌现。

"人生副本"是第一个现象级的AI原生内容形态，但绝对不会是最后一个。

Why Did "Life Copy" Blow Up?

AI short videos aren't new. So why did the "Life Copy" genre specifically catch fire? The answer lies in three key design choices.

First, second-person narrative. Storytelling with "you" versus "he/she" creates a completely different level of immersion. Traditional story videos are "watching someone else's story," but Life Copy is "experiencing someone else's life" — you are the protagonist, you live through everything. That level of代入 is hard to achieve with traditional narrative approaches.

Second, AI-native visual style. It's not live-action, not traditional hand-drawn animation — it's AI-generated comic/illustration style. This style has several advantages: first, extremely low cost, you can generate a high-quality image in minutes; second, consistent style, no issues with varying actor performances; third, precisely because it's not real people, it's actually easier to代入 — you don't get distracted by whether the actor is attractive or not, you just focus on the story itself.

Third, maxed-out emotional value. Life Copy topics mostly focus on themes like "ordinary person's comeback," "loser's rebirth," "making up for regrets" — the things people deep down crave and resonate with most. In real life you might live an ordinary existence, but in these 2-3 minute videos, you can experience a completely different life. The value of this "emotional meal replacement" is especially marketable in an increasingly stressful era.

So you see, this isn't simply "using AI to make videos" — it's an AI-native content format. Its narrative approach, visual style, and production workflow are all custom-built for AI. You simply can't achieve this cost structure or update speed with traditional production thinking.

The Dimension Drop on Content Has Only Just Begun

The viral success of "Life Copy" is just one microcosm of the AI content revolution. Bigger changes are underway.

Start with cost. According to industry data, the average production cycle for traditional 3-minute 2D animation is 18.5 days, costing roughly 8,000 yuan per minute. By 2026, with AI workflows, production time for comparable quality has compressed to under 3 days, and per-minute cost has plummeted to 150 yuan. That's a 6x efficiency gain and a 98% cost reduction. What does that mean? Where a team used to make two videos a month, one person can now make 20.

Then there's barriers to entry. Before, to make short videos you needed shooting skills, editing skills, copywriting skills, maybe acting skills too. Now? If you have ideas and can write prompts, you can generate a pretty decent video. Technical barriers have collapsed; creative barriers have emerged. Going forward, the content industry won't compete on "who has better production tech" — it'll compete on "who has better ideas, who understands user emotions better."

But this also brings a problem: homogenization. When tools become this simple, when everyone's using the same models to generate similar visuals and write similar copy — where does content differentiation come from? The answer might be: personality, vertical depth, unique perspective. Just like in the blogging era, everyone could write — but those who truly broke through were the bloggers with unique style and depth. In the video era, everyone could shoot — but those who went viral were the creators with personal charisma. The AI era is the same — tools let everyone make content, but those who truly stand out are still the ones with unique souls.

Fengqun Culture's 60-account AI matrix, the institutional entry of MCNs — this signals AI content has moved from "individual player experimentation" to "institutionalized operation." Competition will get fiercer from here, but so will opportunities — because the market is expanding rapidly, and new content formats, new platforms, new business models keep emerging.

"Life Copy" is the first phenomenal AI-native content format, but it absolutely won't be the last.
