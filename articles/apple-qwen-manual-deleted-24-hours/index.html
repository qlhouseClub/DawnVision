苹果官网千问手册上线即撤
国行AI落地进入最后阶段

Apple's Qwen Manual Up Then Pulled
China AI Rollout Enters Final Phase

苹果中国官网8月8日上线'配合Apple智能使用千问'手册，8月9日悄然撤下。备案已完成、合作方已表态，删除背后是发布节奏而非合作破裂。

Apple China posts a 'Use Qwen with Apple Intelligence' manual on Aug 8, quietly pulls it Aug 9. Filing is done, partner has confirmed — the deletion signals release timing, not a broken deal.

No.032 2026.08.10 约 5 分钟阅读 ~5 min read

苹果和阿里千问的故事，24小时内来了个大反转。

8月8日，苹果中国官网《Mac使用手册》出现一篇《在Mac上配合Apple智能使用千问》的页面，把怎么设置、怎么调用Siri、怎么用写作工具写得明明白白，甚至给出了适用条件：macOS 26.6或更高版本，针对中国大陆账户、位置以及国行Mac分别说明。8月9日上午，这份手册被悄然撤下，原链接跳回Mac使用手册首页，中文手册恢复显示"配合Apple智能使用ChatGPT"。

于是一个问题瞬间冒出来：苹果和阿里的合作，生变了吗？先别急着下结论。

三块拼图，只有一块被撤下

判断是不是"分手"，要看另外两块拼图。

第一块是监管。7月15日，国家网信办公布新增7款手机端侧生成式人工智能服务备案信息，其中明确包括"Apple智能"——Apple Intelligence在中国大陆推进落地，已经跨过一个重要的合规节点。

第二块是阿里此前的公开表态。7月中旬，阿里方面表示，千问将作为AI能力集成至Apple智能，为中国大陆的iOS、iPadOS、macOS和visionOS用户提供相关能力。也就是说，"苹果+千问"不是那张网页突然制造出来的新故事。

所以，仅凭8月9日删除一份使用手册，得不出"合作取消"的结论。更值得注意的是这份手册的颗粒度：它已经具体到系统版本、账户条件、设置路径和Siri调用方式，远超一句普通宣传语的精度。这更像产品准备已走得相当靠前，而正式开放的时间、系统版本或发布节奏仍存在调整。

监管备案完成、合作方表态确认、使用说明一度上线——苹果中国AI落地，已经进入一个非常敏感的最后阶段。苹果至今没有解释为什么删除页面，这种沉默本身，就是最大的信号。

千问要的不是用户，是系统入口

抛开"黄没黄"的争论，这场波折真正值得关注的是：国产大模型争夺的战场，已经从"谁的App下载量高"，推进到了"谁能进入操作系统和硬件入口"。

如果AI模型进入Siri、写作工具乃至未来更多系统场景，用户甚至不需要主动打开某个AI应用。对大模型厂商而言，这种入口的价值，远高于手机桌面上多一个图标——站在Siri背后，获得的是一次次真实的系统级调用，而不是可被随时卸载的流量。

这才是"苹果选谁当中国AI伙伴"被高度关注的原因。苹果卖的不只是iPhone和Mac，它拥有一整套由硬件、操作系统、应用生态组成的超级入口。谁成为这套系统里的AI底座，谁就拿到了中国数亿智能终端背后的对话权。

过去我们讨论国产AI，总在比较谁的模型跑分高几分。苹果中国AI的推进提供了另一个观察角度：中国大模型已经开始参与全球顶级消费电子生态的底层能力分工。未来国产大模型的真正座次，可能越来越取决于三件事：能不能进入超级终端，能不能承受亿级用户调用，能不能把模型能力真正变成系统级服务。

所以，8月9日删除的这份网页，不该被简单解读成一场合作突然"黄了"。恰恰相反，它让一个更大的悬念浮出水面：备案已经完成，合作方已经表态，手册甚至已经短暂出现——现在市场真正等的，只剩苹果什么时候把最后那扇门正式打开。当那一天到来，中国大模型争夺的，就不再只是聊天机器人市场，而是中国数亿智能终端背后的AI入口。

明天见。

The Apple–Qwen story flipped on its head within 24 hours.

On August 8, Apple China's Mac User Guide showed a page titled "Use Qwen with Apple Intelligence on Mac," complete with setup steps, Siri invocation, writing tools, and even the fine print: macOS 26.6 or later, with separate guidance for mainland China accounts, locations, and China-market Macs. On the morning of August 9, the manual quietly vanished; the link redirected to the guide's homepage, and the Chinese manual reverted to "Use ChatGPT with Apple Intelligence."

A question immediately surfaced: is the Apple–Alibaba deal in trouble? Hold that thought.

Three Pieces of the Puzzle — Only One Got Pulled

To judge whether this is a "breakup," look at the other two pieces.

Piece one is regulation. On July 15, the Cyberspace Administration published filings for seven new on-device generative AI services — "Apple Intelligence" was explicitly among them. Apple Intelligence's mainland rollout has already crossed a major compliance milestone.

Piece two is Alibaba's own public statement. In mid-July, Alibaba said Qwen would be integrated into Apple Intelligence as an AI capability for mainland iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and visionOS users. In other words, "Apple + Qwen" wasn't a story invented by that webpage.

So deleting one user manual on August 9 doesn't warrant a "deal cancelled" conclusion. What's more telling is the manual's granularity: system versions, account conditions, setup paths, Siri invocation — precision far beyond a marketing line. This looks like product preparation that ran far ahead, while official release timing, system versions, or rollout rhythm are still being adjusted.

Regulatory filing done, partner confirmed, user guide briefly live — Apple China AI is in a highly sensitive final phase. Apple hasn't explained the deletion. That silence, in itself, is the biggest signal.

Qwen Isn't After Users. It's After the System Entry Point

Beyond the "is it dead or alive" debate, what this episode really shows: the battlefield for Chinese LLMs has moved from "whose app has more downloads" to "who gets into the operating system and hardware entry point."

If an AI model sits inside Siri, writing tools, and more system scenarios to come, users never need to open a standalone AI app. For an LLM vendor, that entry point's value dwarfs another icon on the home screen — standing behind Siri means real system-level invocations, not traffic that can be uninstalled at will.

That's why "who Apple picks as its China AI partner" gets so much attention. Apple doesn't just sell iPhones and Macs; it owns a super entry point built from hardware, OS, and app ecosystem. Whoever becomes the AI foundation inside that system gets the conversation rights behind hundreds of millions of Chinese smart devices.

We used to compare Chinese LLMs by benchmark margins. Apple's China AI push offers another lens: Chinese models are already participating in the underlying capability division of the world's top consumer-electronics ecosystem. The future pecking order of Chinese LLMs may hinge on three things: entering super terminals, absorbing billion-user load, and turning model capability into true system-level services.

So that deleted page shouldn't be read as a suddenly dead deal. Quite the opposite — it surfaces a bigger question: the filing is done, the partner has confirmed, the manual even briefly appeared. What the market is really waiting for is Apple finally opening that last door. When that day comes, the prize isn't the chatbot market. It's the AI entry point behind hundreds of millions of Chinese devices.
