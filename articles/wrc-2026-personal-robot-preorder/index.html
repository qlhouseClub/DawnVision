2026世界机器人大会在北京闭幕 消费级人形机器人开启预订9月发货

World Robot Conference 2026 Concludes in Beijing Consumer Humanoids Open Pre-orders, September Shipping

373家企业参展、150+新品首发，上纬新材启元机器人开启预订，银河通用发布双足人形——具身智能to C拐点已至。

373 companies, 150+ debuts. Shangwei Qiyuan robots open pre-orders, Galbot unveils bipedal humanoid — the consumer embodied AI inflection point is here.

No.042 2026.08.24 约 5 分钟阅读 ~5 min read

会烤披萨、会做按摩、会打网球——8月23日，2026世界机器人大会（WRC 2026）在北京亦庄闭幕。五天展期，373家国内外企业带来了超过3000件创新产品，其中首发新品突破150件。

但真正值得记住的，不是展馆里那些酷炫的demo，而是一个信号：家庭消费级机器人，终于开始接受预订了。

上纬新材旗下智元机器人品牌"启元"在大会期间宣布，Q1和T1两款消费级人形机器人正式开启预订，首批产品9月份发货。Q1身高仅88厘米、重量不到15公斤，定位家庭陪伴和轻量家务场景，还拿了国际A'Design金奖。这不是实验室里的原型机，不是需要百万预算的工业机器人——这是普通消费者真的可以预订、真的能抱回家的产品。

从"秀场"到"卖场"

前几年的世界机器人大会，更像是一个"科技秀场"。机器人会跳舞、会翻跟头、会做酷炫的动作，但你问"这东西什么时候能买到？"，得到的答案永远是"还在研发中""商用还需要时间"。

今年不一样了。你在展馆里看到的很多产品，旁边就放着二维码，扫码就能预订、就能问价格、就能了解发货时间。烤披萨的机器人、做咖啡的机器人、打扫卫生的机器人、陪孩子写作业的机器人——它们不再是"未来概念"，而是正在变成商品。

银河通用在大会期间发布了双足人形机器人Galbot ET1，同时展示了自研的具身大模型"银河星脑"。这家公司因为春晚"小盖"机器人而被大众熟知，现在正在把技术从舞台搬到真实场景。宇树科技的Unitree Go2四足机器人已经卖了几万台，新发布的H1人形机器人也开始小批量交付。

这是具身智能行业的一个关键转折点：从"展示技术"转向"交付产品"。

家庭机器人的真问题

当然，消费级机器人要真正走进千家万户，还有很多问题要解决。

第一个问题是价格。现在的消费级人形机器人价格还在几万到十几万人民币区间，相当于一辆家用轿车的价格，大多数家庭不会为一个"能做简单家务的玩具"掏这个钱。价格降到万元以内，才有可能真正爆发。

第二个问题是实用性。今天的家庭机器人能做的事情还非常有限——能打招呼、能走两步、能拿个轻的东西，但真要让它做饭、打扫卫生、照顾老人，还差得远。机器人的手眼协调、精细操作、长期自主导航，这些技术问题还没有完全解决。

第三个问题是安全性。机器人在家里跑，有老人有孩子，万一撞到人、摔了东西、出了故障怎么办？安全标准、认证体系、保险机制，这些软件层面的东西比硬件更难。

但方向是明确的。就像2007年的iPhone、2014年的特斯拉，第一代产品肯定不完美，但只要有人开始买、开始用、开始反馈，迭代速度就会指数级加快。从"能预订"到"很好用"，可能只需要两三年时间。

具身智能的故事讲了很多年，今年终于开始落地了。明年的WRC，我们可能会看到第一波真实用户的使用反馈——那才是真正有意思的时候。

明天见。

Making pizza, giving massages, playing tennis — on August 23, the 2026 World Robot Conference (WRC 2026) concluded in Beijing's Yizhuang district. Over five days, 373 domestic and international companies showcased over 3,000 innovative products, with more than 150 making their global debut.

But what's truly worth remembering isn't the flashy demos on the show floor — it's one signal: consumer home robots are finally taking pre-orders.

During the conference, Shangwei Materials' Zhiyuan Robotics brand "Qiyuan" announced that two consumer humanoid robots, Q1 and T1, are officially open for pre-order, with the first batch shipping in September. The Q1 stands just 88cm tall and weighs under 15kg, positioned for home companionship and light household tasks. It even won an international A'Design Gold Award. This isn't a lab prototype or an industrial robot requiring a seven-figure budget — this is a product ordinary consumers can actually pre-order and bring home.

From "Demo Theater" to "Sales Floor"

At previous World Robot Conferences, the event felt more like a "tech show." Robots would dance, do backflips, perform cool stunts — but if you asked "when can I buy this?" the answer was always "still in R&D" or "commercialization will take time."

This year was different. Next to many products on display were QR codes — scan to pre-order, ask about pricing, get shipping estimates. Pizza-making robots, coffee robots, cleaning robots, homework-tutoring robots — they're no longer "future concepts." They're becoming products.

Galaxy General unveiled its bipedal humanoid robot Galbot ET1 at the conference, alongside its self-developed embodied large model "Galaxy Brain." The company became widely known for its "Xiao Gai" robot appearance on the Spring Festival Gala, and is now moving its technology from the stage to real-world scenarios. Unitree's Go2 quadruped has already sold tens of thousands of units, and the newly released H1 humanoid has started small-batch delivery.

This marks a critical inflection point for the embodied AI industry: shifting from "showcasing technology" to "shipping products."

The Real Problems for Home Robots

Of course, consumer robots still have many problems to solve before they truly enter millions of homes.

The first is price. Today's consumer humanoids are still priced in the tens to hundreds of thousands of RMB range — comparable to a family car. Most households won't spend that on a "toy that does simple chores." Prices need to drop below 10,000 RMB for mass adoption to happen.

The second is utility. What home robots can actually do today is still very limited — greet people, walk around, pick up light objects. But truly cooking, cleaning, or caring for the elderly? Not there yet. Hand-eye coordination, fine manipulation, and long-term autonomous navigation are still unsolved technical problems.

The third is safety. With robots running around homes with elderly people and children, what happens if it bumps into someone, drops something, or malfunctions? Safety standards, certification systems, and insurance mechanisms — these "software" layers are harder than the hardware itself.

But the direction is clear. Just like the iPhone in 2007 or Tesla in 2014, first-generation products are never perfect. But once people start buying, using, and giving feedback, iteration speed accelerates exponentially. From "available for pre-order" to "genuinely useful" may only take two or three years.

The embodied AI story has been told for years. This year, it's finally starting to materialize. At next year's WRC, we might see the first wave of real user feedback — and that's when it gets really interesting.
