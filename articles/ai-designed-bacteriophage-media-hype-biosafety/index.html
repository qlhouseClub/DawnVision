AI造噬菌体被媒体吹成末日病毒
经典hype三板斧又来了

AI Phage Framed as Doomsday Virus by Media
The Classic Three-Axe Hype Routine Returns

AI设计噬菌体的科研成果，被媒体包装成"AI造超级病毒""打开潘多拉魔盒"。噬菌体是杀细菌的，不感染人类，是用来治病的。经典的AI焦虑营销，又开张了。

Scientific research on AI-designed bacteriophages gets framed by media as "AI creates supervirus" and "opening Pandora's box." Phages kill bacteria, don't infect humans, and are used to treat disease. Classic AI anxiety marketing strikes again.

No.033 2026.08.12 约 4 分钟阅读 ~4 min read

朋友们，今天又有媒体来给大家表演"AI末日文学"了。

事情是这样的：斯坦福和ARC研究所的团队在《自然·生物技术》上发了篇论文，说他们用基因组语言模型从头设计了噬菌体病毒，302条候选里有16条成功实现了自我复制。研究目的很明确——对付耐药性细菌，做噬菌体疗法。简单说就是：用AI设计出只杀细菌的病毒，来拯救那些抗生素已经杀不死的超级细菌感染者。

这明明是件好事对吧？你猜某些媒体的标题怎么写的？

"震惊！AI首次设计出病毒，人类打开了潘多拉魔盒！"

"AI造出超级病毒，生物安全再亮红灯！"

"从零造命：AI写病毒基因组，生化危机离我们还有多远？"

我差点以为AI已经造出了能毁灭人类的T病毒了。翻到论文一看——哦，噬菌体啊。噬菌体，就是只感染细菌、不感染人类细胞的那种病毒。它的存在就是为了杀细菌的，而且人家在自然界里已经存在了几十亿年，数量比细菌还多。你喝一口水，里面就有上亿个噬菌体。

经典hype三板斧，一个都没少

朋友们，这种AI生物新闻的标题党套路，我见过太多次了，堪称经典三板斧。

第一斧：偷换概念，把"噬菌体"换成"病毒"。普通人一听"病毒"就想到新冠、艾滋、埃博拉，瞬间代入"致命传染病"的恐惧。但噬菌体是什么？是细菌的病毒，对人类细胞完全不感兴趣。这就好比你说"科学家设计出了一种新的杀虫剂"，然后媒体标题写成"科学家造出致命毒药"——不能说完全不对，但那个味儿全歪了。

第二斧：模糊背景，绝口不提是用来治病的。为什么要研究噬菌体疗法？因为抗生素耐药性已经成了全球公共卫生危机，每年有几百万人死于耐药菌感染，现有的抗生素越来越不管用了。噬菌体疗法是最有希望的替代方案之一。AI加速噬菌体设计，本质上是在加速救命药的研发。但媒体不管，只告诉你"AI造病毒了"，不告诉你这病毒是用来救人的。

第三斧："从零造命"，往"生命起源"的宏大叙事上靠。什么"AI第一次从零设计生命"啊，"人类扮演上帝"啊，说得玄乎其玄。先不说病毒到底算不算生命科学界还在吵架，就算算吧——这不是从零设计一个新物种，这是用语言模型在已知噬菌体的基础上做设计和优化，最后还要在实验室里合成、验证、筛选。302条候选里只有16条活了，成功率5%都不到。这水平离"扮演上帝"还差着十万八千里呢。

真正的风险被掩盖了

说真的，我不是在否认AI合成生物学的潜在风险——风险确实存在，需要认真对待。但风险不在AI模型本身，而在实验室管理和监管规则上。

你想想，一个有恶意的人，用不用AI他都能合成危险的东西——基因合成技术已经很成熟了，你只要有钱，找个外包公司就能合成你想要的基因序列。AI降低了设计门槛是事实，但最关键的瓶颈从来不是设计，是合成和实验条件。而且噬菌体疗法的研究是公开的、受到监管的，成果是用来治病救人的——这和"AI制造生物武器"完全是两码事。

真正需要警惕的是什么？是生物实验的准入门槛越来越低，是基因合成服务的监管还不够完善，是双重用途研究的国际规则还不清晰。这些才是真正的生物安全问题，不是哪个AI模型的问题。把锅甩给AI，反而让大家忽略了真正需要解决的问题。

但媒体才不管这些。"AI设计噬菌体疗法应对抗生素耐药性"——这种标题有谁会点？"AI造出超级病毒，生化危机即将到来"——点击率直接拉满。反正吓的是读者，涨的是KPI，至于公众对AI和生物技术的认知会不会被带歪，谁在乎呢？

朋友们，下次看到"AI造生命""AI造病毒""AI设计末日武器"之类的标题，先深呼吸三秒钟，然后问自己三个问题：第一，这个东西是感染什么的？细菌还是人？第二，它本来是用来干嘛的？治病还是搞破坏？第三，成功率是多少？是100%还是5%？

问完这三个问题，你就会发现：绝大多数"AI末日新闻"，其实都是包装成恐惧的KPI。

今天就槽到这里，明天继续。

Friends, today yet another media outlet is here to perform "AI doomsday literature" for us.

Here's what actually happened: a team from Stanford and the ARC Institute published a paper in Nature Biotechnology saying they used a genome language model to design bacteriophage viruses from scratch — out of 302 candidates, 16 successfully achieved self-replication. The research goal is clear: to fight antibiotic-resistant bacteria, to develop phage therapy. Put simply: use AI to design viruses that only kill bacteria, to save people infected with superbugs that antibiotics can no longer stop.

This is obviously a good thing, right? Guess how some media outlets headlined it?

"Shocking! AI Designs Virus for the First Time — Humanity Opens Pandora's Box!"

"AI Creates Supervirus, Biosecurity Red Light Flashes Again!"

"Creating Life from Scratch: AI Writes Viral Genomes — How Far Is Bio-Crisis?"

I almost thought AI had created a T-virus that could wipe out humanity. Then I looked at the paper — oh, bacteriophages. Bacteriophages — the kind of virus that only infects bacteria, not human cells. They exist to kill bacteria, and they've been around for billions of years in nature, more numerous than bacteria themselves. Take a sip of water and there are hundreds of millions of phages in it.

The Classic Three-Axe Hype Playbook, All Present and Accounted For

Friends, I've seen this clickbait formula in AI biology news way too many times — it's the classic three-axe routine.

Axe one: swap the concept, replace 'bacteriophage' with 'virus.' Regular people hear "virus" and immediately think COVID, HIV, Ebola — instant flashback to "deadly pandemic" fears. But what's a bacteriophage? It's a virus of bacteria. It couldn't care less about human cells. It's like saying "scientists design a new pesticide" and the media headline reads "scientists create deadly poison" — not technically wrong, but the entire vibe is skewed.

Axe two: bury the context, never mention it's for treating disease. Why research phage therapy? Because antibiotic resistance has become a global public health crisis — millions of people die every year from drug-resistant bacterial infections, and existing antibiotics are working less and less. Phage therapy is one of the most promising alternative approaches. AI accelerating phage design essentially means accelerating the development of life-saving medicine. But the media doesn't care — they just tell you "AI made a virus" and don't tell you it's made to save lives.

Axe three: "creating life from scratch," leaning into the grand narrative of "the origin of life." Stuff like "AI designs life for the first time," "humanity playing God" — made to sound all mystical and profound. Leaving aside whether viruses even count as life (a debate still raging in the scientific community), even if they do — this isn't designing a new species from zero. It's using language models to design and optimize based on known phages, then synthesizing, validating, and screening them in a lab. Only 16 out of 302 candidates worked — less than 5% success rate. That level is still leagues away from "playing God."

The Real Risks Get Buried

To be clear, I'm not denying the potential risks of AI synthetic biology — risks exist, and they need to be taken seriously. But the risk isn't in the AI model itself; it's in lab management and regulatory frameworks.

Think about it. A malicious person can synthesize dangerous things with or without AI — gene synthesis technology is already quite mature. If you have money, you can find a contract company to synthesize whatever gene sequence you want. It's true that AI lowers the barrier to design, but the biggest bottleneck was never design — it's synthesis and experimental conditions. And phage therapy research is public and regulated; its purpose is to cure and save people — that's a completely different category from "AI building bioweapons."

What actually deserves vigilance? It's that the barrier to entry for biological experiments is getting lower and lower, that regulation of gene synthesis services isn't robust enough, that international rules for dual-use research remain unclear. These are the real biosecurity issues — not problems with any AI model. Blaming it on AI instead makes everyone ignore the problems that actually need solving.

But the media could care less. "AI designs phage therapy to fight antibiotic resistance" — who clicks on that title? "AI creates supervirus, bio-crisis imminent" — click-through rate goes straight through the roof. The readers get scared, the KPIs go up, and whether the public's understanding of AI and biotechnology gets distorted? Who cares?

Friends, next time you see headlines like "AI creates life," "AI builds a virus," "AI designs doomsday weapons" — take three deep breaths, then ask yourself three questions: first, what does this thing infect? Bacteria or humans? Second, what was it originally made for — healing or harming? Third, what's the success rate — 100% or 5%?

After asking those three questions, you'll realize: the vast majority of "AI doomsday news" is just KPI wrapped in fear.

That's all the roasting for today. More tomorrow.
