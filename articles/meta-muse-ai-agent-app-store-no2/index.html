Meta终于正式入局AI Agent战场了。

9月9日，Meta发布了全新的AI Agent应用Muse，定位是"帮你处理杂务的AI助手"——可以帮你网购、写邮件、规划旅行、管理日程。两天后，根据市场情报公司Sensor Tower的数据，Muse在美区iOS的下载量超过83,000次，登上了App Store第2名。

数字背后：No.2 vs 实际热度

App Store第2名听起来很厉害，但需要拆开看。

83,000次下载是什么概念？Meta自家的Threads在2023年首日下载量是430万。ChatGPT在2022年底上线时，日均下载量约为83,000次——和Muse两天的总量持平。这意味着Muse的热度远不及Meta自家的Threads，也比不上ChatGPT首发时的势头。

更值得注意的是，Muse目前仅限美国市场，且Android端表现很弱——在Google Play生产力类排名仅第338位。Web和WhatsApp端的使用数据尚未纳入统计。

Muse的差异化：Agentic不只是聊天

与ChatGPT、Claude等对话式AI不同，Muse的定位是主动式Agent。它不只是回答问题，而是可以主动替你执行任务。

The Verge的编辑在体验后评价："Muse确实能帮我处理一些杂务，但有时它的主动介入会让人感到不安。"这种"creepy but useful"的评价，可能代表了消费者对AI Agent的普遍心态：我们想要AI帮忙，但不想让AI太"主动"。

Meta的战略：从社交到Agent

Muse的发布标志着Meta的战略重心正在从社交媒体转向AI Agent。

过去两年，Meta在AI上的投入主要集中在两个方向：一是为Facebook、Instagram、WhatsApp提供AI功能（推荐、广告、内容审核），二是开发开源模型（Llama系列）。Muse是Meta第一次在消费端推出独立的AI Agent产品。

这个方向是对的。当OpenAI、Anthropic、Google都在争夺Agent平台的主导权时，Meta的优势在于庞大的用户基数和社交图谱。如果Muse能接入Facebook、Instagram、WhatsApp的数据和功能，它的Agent能力将远超其他竞品。

AI Agent的消费端挑战

Muse的开局数据也暴露了AI Agent在消费端的挑战。

消费者对AI Agent的需求是真实的——谁不想有个AI帮自己处理杂务？但信任是最大的障碍。让AI替你网购、写邮件、管理日程，意味着你要把自己的个人信息、支付权限、社交关系交给AI。这需要极高的信任度。

目前，大多数消费者对AI的信任还停留在"对话助手"阶段。要跨越到"Agent"阶段，还需要时间。

Muse的83,000次下载，与其说是"成功"，不如说是"起步"。真正的考验是留存率和使用深度——用户下载之后会不会持续使用？会不会真正把任务交给Agent？

明天见。

Meta has finally entered the AI agent battlefield.

On September 9, Meta launched its new AI agent app Muse, positioned as an "AI assistant that handles your busywork" — it can help with online shopping, emails, trip planning, and schedule management. Two days later, according to market intelligence firm Sensor Tower, Muse hit over 83,000 iOS downloads in the US, reaching No. 2 on the App Store.

Behind the Numbers: No. 2 vs Actual Heat

App Store No. 2 sounds impressive, but let's break it down.

What does 83,000 downloads mean? Meta's own Threads hit 4.3 million first-day downloads in 2023. ChatGPT averaged about 83,000 daily downloads when it launched in late 2022 — matching Muse's two-day total. This means Muse's popularity is far below Meta's own Threads and doesn't match ChatGPT's launch momentum.

More notably, Muse is currently limited to the US market, and its Android performance is weak — ranking only No. 338 in Google Play's Productivity category. Web and WhatsApp usage data aren't included in these estimates.

Muse's Differentiation: Agentic Means More Than Chat

Unlike conversational AIs like ChatGPT and Claude, Muse is positioned as a proactive agent. It doesn't just answer questions; it can proactively execute tasks on your behalf.

The Verge's editor noted after testing: "Muse can indeed handle some tasks for me, but sometimes its proactive involvement feels unsettling." This "creepy but useful" assessment may represent consumers' general attitude toward AI agents: we want AI help, but we don't want AI to be too 'proactive.'

Meta's Strategy: From Social to Agent

Muse's launch marks Meta's strategic shift from social media to AI agents.

Over the past two years, Meta's AI investments have focused on two directions: providing AI features for Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp (recommendations, ads, content moderation), and developing open-source models (Llama series). Muse is Meta's first independent AI agent product in the consumer space.

The direction is right. As OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google compete for agent platform dominance, Meta's advantage lies in its massive user base and social graph. If Muse can tap into Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp data and features, its agent capabilities will far surpass competitors.

AI Agent's Consumer Challenge

Muse's initial data also reveals AI agent challenges in the consumer space.

Consumer demand for AI agents is real — who wouldn't want an AI handling their busywork? But trust is the biggest barrier. Letting AI shop online, write emails, and manage your schedule means handing over personal information, payment permissions, and social connections to AI. This requires extremely high trust levels.

Currently, most consumers' trust in AI remains at the "conversational assistant" stage. Crossing to the "agent" stage will take time.

Muse's 83,000 downloads represent less a "success" than a "starting point." The real test is retention and depth of use — will users keep using it after downloading? Will they actually delegate tasks to the agent?
