133亿美元。半年前，这个数字还是66亿。

欧洲AI编程公司Lovable完成了C轮融资——4亿美元，估值升至133亿美元。Menlo Ventures和Scaleup Europe Fund领投，十余家机构参与。从去年12月融资时的66亿估值，到现在的133亿，只用了不到一年时间。

这个故事的重点不是又一个AI公司估值涨了——这种新闻现在每天都有。重点在于：Lovable是一家欧洲公司。

过去的AI创业叙事，几乎就是硅谷叙事。OpenAI在旧金山，Anthropic在旧金山，Cognition在旧金山，Cursor的总部也在美国。AI的创新中心、资本中心、人才中心——似乎都集中在那一小块湾区。

但Lovable的故事告诉你：AI创业的地理红利，正在从单点扩散到全球。

从单点爆发到多点开花

为什么Lovable值得关注？不是因为它估值有多高——SpaceX收购Cursor是600亿，Cognition传要融到400亿，133亿跟它们比算不上最顶级。但Lovable代表的是一个趋势：AI编程创业不再只是硅谷的独家游戏。

看看全球AI编程赛道的玩家分布：

美国有OpenAI的Codex、Anthropic的Claude Code、SpaceX的Cursor、Cognition的Devin——这些是第一梯队，也是最受关注的。

中国有DeepSeek的Harness、智谱的CodeGeeX、字节的豆包编程助手——依托国内庞大的开发者市场和成本优势，增长速度极快。

而欧洲呢？过去欧洲在AI领域的存在感不算强。大模型没有特别突出的，AI产品也多是美国公司的翻版。但Lovable的出现改变了这个叙事——欧洲不仅有了自己的AI编程独角兽，而且估值已经进入了全球第一梯队的门槛。

"当欧洲也跑出百亿级AI编程公司的时候，你就知道这场创业潮已经从硅谷的野火，变成了全球的燎原之势。"—— 一位关注全球AI创业的投资人

这背后有几个原因。第一，AI工具的普及降低了创业门槛。以前做一个编程工具公司，需要一支庞大的工程团队和巨额研发投入。现在有了AI，几个创始人用AI辅助开发，就能在很短时间内做出有竞争力的产品。

第二，远程工作的普及让人才不再集中。过去你想做AI创业，必须搬到硅谷才能招到人。现在全球的开发者都可以远程协作，伦敦、柏林、巴黎、布拉格——任何一个有技术人才的城市，都能长出AI创业公司。

第三，资本的全球化。美国的VC愿意投欧洲的公司，欧洲的基金也在投美国的项目。资本的流动不再受地理边界限制，哪里有好项目，钱就去哪里。

对普通创业者意味着什么？

Lovable的故事，对那些不在硅谷、不在北上广深的普通创业者来说，是一个重要的信号。

以前的创业逻辑是：要做大事，必须去中心城市、必须进顶级圈子、必须认识大佬。地理决定了你能接触到的资源，也决定了你事业的天花板。

但AI时代，这个逻辑正在被改写。

你不需要在硅谷，也能做出估值百亿美元的公司。你不需要有豪华的创始团队背景，也能靠产品本身说话。你不需要认识投资人，也能通过产品的自然增长拿到融资。

关键是什么？是产品力。在AI领域，好产品自己会说话——开发者社区会传播，用户会付费，投资人会找上门。地理的壁垒，正在被数字时代的连接能力一点点瓦解。

当然，这不是说地理完全不重要了。硅谷仍然有最密集的人才、最活跃的资本、最成熟的生态。中国的一线城市也有类似的优势。但是——

如果你是一个在二三线城市、在欧洲小镇、在任何一个有网络的地方的独立开发者，Lovable的故事告诉你：你也有机会。不是安慰自己的那种"机会"，是真的能做出十亿美元级产品的机会。

AI创业的门槛降低了，但成功率并没有提高。能跑出来的永远是少数。但至少，这个游戏不再只是少数人、少数地方的特权了。

当AI编程的独角兽从硅谷、北京、欧洲甚至更多地方冒出来的时候，这场革命才算真正开始。

$13.3 billion. Six months ago, that number was $6.6 billion.

European AI coding company Lovable has closed its Series C - $400 million, at a $13.3 billion valuation. Menlo Ventures and Scaleup Europe Fund led the round, with over a dozen institutions participating. From $6.6B at last December's funding round to $13.3B now took less than a year.

The point of this story isn't just another AI company's valuation going up - that kind of news happens every day now. The point is: Lovable is a European company.

The AI startup narrative used to be essentially a Silicon Valley narrative. OpenAI in San Francisco, Anthropic in San Francisco, Cognition in San Francisco, Cursor also US-based. AI's innovation center, capital center, talent center - all seemed concentrated in that one small patch of the Bay Area.

But Lovable's story tells you: the geographic dividend of AI entrepreneurship is spreading from a single point to the entire globe.

From Single-Point Explosion to Global Bloom

Why does Lovable matter? Not because its valuation is the highest - SpaceX acquired Cursor for $60B, Cognition is reportedly raising at $40B - $13.3B isn't the very top tier. But Lovable represents a trend: AI coding startup is no longer Silicon Valley's exclusive game.

Look at the global AI coding player map:

In the US: OpenAI's Codex, Anthropic's Claude Code, SpaceX's Cursor, Cognition's Devin - the first tier, and the most watched.

In China: DeepSeek's Harness, Zhipu's CodeGeeX, ByteDance's Doubao Coding Assistant - leveraging the massive domestic developer market and cost advantages, growing extremely fast.

And Europe? In the past, Europe hasn't had a strong presence in AI. No standout foundation models, AI products were mostly copies of American ones. But Lovable changes that narrative - Europe now has its own AI coding unicorn, and its valuation has already crossed the threshold into the global first tier.

"When Europe also produces a $10B+ AI coding company, you know this startup wave has gone from a Silicon Valley wildfire to a global blaze." - A Global AI Startup Investor

Several reasons drive this. First, AI tools have lowered the barrier to entrepreneurship. Building a coding tools company used to require a huge engineering team and massive R&D investment. Now with AI, a few founders with AI-assisted development can ship competitive products in very little time.

Second, remote work has decentralized talent. Previously, to do AI startups you had to move to Silicon Valley to hire. Now developers worldwide can collaborate remotely - London, Berlin, Paris, Prague - any city with technical talent can grow AI startups.

Third, globalized capital. American VCs invest in European companies; European funds back American projects. Capital flows no longer respect geographic borders. Wherever the good projects are, the money follows.

What It Means for Ordinary Founders

Lovable's story is an important signal for ordinary founders who aren't in Silicon Valley or tier-1 Chinese cities.

The old startup logic was: to do big things, you had to be in a hub city, you had to be in the right circles, you had to know the right people. Geography determined your access to resources, and it determined your ceiling.

But in the AI era, that logic is being rewritten.

You don't need to be in Silicon Valley to build a $10B+ company. You don't need a fancy founding team background - the product speaks for itself. You don't need to know investors - organic growth from a good product brings them to you.

The key? Product-market fit. In AI, good products spread on their own - developer communities talk, users pay, investors come knocking. Geographic barriers are being eroded, bit by bit, by the connectivity of the digital age.

Of course, this isn't to say geography doesn't matter at all. Silicon Valley still has the densest talent, the most active capital, the most mature ecosystem. China's tier-1 cities have similar advantages. But -

If you're an indie developer in a second-tier city, in a small European town, anywhere with internet - Lovable's story tells you: you have a shot. Not the comforting kind of "shot," but a real shot at building something billion-dollar-scale.

The bar for AI startups has lowered, but the success rate hasn't improved. Only a few ever make it. But at least the game is no longer the privilege of a few people in a few places.

When AI coding unicorns emerge from Silicon Valley, Beijing, Europe, and beyond - that's when this revolution truly begins.
