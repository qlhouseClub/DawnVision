Google AI搜索达43%渗透率

Google AI Search Hits 43%
From Blue Links to AI Destination

Similarweb报告：AI Overviews一年从15%飙至43%搜索覆盖，AI Mode月访问2.79亿。出版商流量被截但引用增5倍，Google正从"互联网入口"变成"回答终点"。

Similarweb: AI Overviews surges from 15% to 43% of searches in one year; AI Mode hits 279M monthly visits. Publishers lose traffic but AI citations grow 5x. Google is transforming from 'gateway to the web' to 'destination for answers.'

你有多久没点击过Google搜索结果的第二页了？如果最近连第一页的蓝色链接都越点越少了，那你已经是AI搜索时代的用户了。

市场情报公司Similarweb发布的《2026生成式AI格局》报告给出了一组惊人的数据：Google的AI生成回答功能AI Overviews在仅仅一年时间里，从出现在15%的搜索结果中飙升到43%。也就是说，现在将近一半的Google搜索，用户看到的不再是蓝色链接列表，而是AI直接生成的答案。

从"帮你找"到"直接答"：搜索的范式转移

一年前，AI Overviews还只是搜索页面顶部的一个"AI层"——你可以看AI的总结，也可以继续往下滑点链接。现在，AI Overviews已经成为搜索旅程本身的一部分：用户先看到AI答案，然后可以通过更具对话性的AI Mode继续深入研究，而不必离开Google。

数据支撑了这个趋势：AI Mode的月访问量从2025年6月的1.26亿增长到2026年5月的2.79亿，翻了一倍还多。用户在Google上的平均搜索时长也在增加，说明人们正在用更长的自然语言对话式查询取代短关键词搜索——这正是AI对话式搜索鼓励的行为。

这个变化对Google来说是好消息——用户在Google平台上停留的时间更长了，Google不再只是通往其他网站的入口，它自己就是目的地。但对依赖搜索引荐流量的出版商和内容创作者来说，这是一个生存级别的挑战。

出版商的困境：被截胡的流量和不够用的引用

过去一年，出版商的哀嚎声此起彼伏。Similarweb去年就报道过AI Overviews对新闻出版商流量的毁灭性打击——用户在Google上直接看到了答案，就没有动力再点击链接去原文了。Cloudflare在7月1日推出了新政策和工具，允许出版商屏蔽AI爬虫或通过其市场向AI公司收费访问内容，本质上是在给内容创作者提供武器反抗。

但数据呈现出复杂的画面。虽然AI回答导致直接点击率下降，但AI回答中包含引用链接的比例在过去一年增长了5倍以上。也就是说，AI越来越多地在回答中标注信息来源。这对出版商来说是一线希望——如果AI的回答能带来精准的高意向流量，即使总量少了，质量可能更高。

不过引用率的绝对水平仍然很低。截至2026年5月，美国桌面端ChatGPT查询中只有6.8%包含引用链接（旅游、零售和体育类稍好）。Google没有公布自己的引用率数据，但从Similarweb的监测来看，Google的AI Overviews引用比例比ChatGPT高，但仍然远低于100%。

一个积极信号是：在5月7日搜索更新后，ChatGPT对网页的引荐比例从2026年3月的25%翻倍到了5月底的近60%。这说明当AI搜索结果中的蓝色链接更醒目时，用户确实会点击——人们不是不想看原文，而是AI界面把原文藏得太深了。

内容创作者的新生存法则

面对搜索从"入口"到"目的地"的转变，内容创作者需要重新思考自己的定位。

如果你的内容只是在回答事实性问题（"什么是X""X的步骤是什么"），那AI Overviews会直接替代你——因为AI可以更快速、更便捷地给出同样的答案。但如果你的内容提供独特观点、深度分析、一手经验、社区归属感——这些是AI生成不了的，也是AI回答会引用但无法替代的。

换句话说，"信息提供者"的角色在贬值，"观点塑造者"和"体验构建者"的价值在上升。这不是内容的末日，而是内容的升级——只不过升级的方向，是往更难、更独特、更有人味的方向走。

当搜索不再需要点击，能让人主动找上门的内容，才是真正有价值的内容。

明天见。

How long has it been since you clicked through to page two of Google search results? If recently you've been clicking fewer and fewer blue links even on page one, you're already a user of the AI search era.

Market intelligence firm Similarweb's "2026 Generative AI Landscape" report delivers striking numbers: Google's AI-generated answers feature, AI Overviews, surged from appearing in 15% of searches to 43% in just one year. That means nearly half of all Google searches now show users AI-generated answers directly, rather than a list of blue links.

From 'Help You Find' to 'Answer Directly': The Search Paradigm Shift

A year ago, AI Overviews was just an "AI layer" on top of search — you could read the AI summary or keep scrolling to click links. Now, AI Overviews has become an integral part of the search journey itself: users see the AI answer first, then can continue deeper research through the more conversational AI Mode without ever leaving Google.

The data backs up this trend: AI Mode monthly visits grew from 126 million in June 2025 to 279 million in May 2026, more than doubling. Average search session length on Google is also increasing, indicating people are replacing short keyword searches with longer, natural-language conversational queries — exactly the behavior AI conversational search encourages.

This change is great news for Google — users spend more time on Google's platform, and Google is no longer just a gateway to other websites; it is the destination itself. But for publishers and content creators who depend on search referral traffic, it's an existential challenge.

Publishers' Dilemma: Siphoned Traffic and Insufficient Citations

Publishers have been howling for the past year. Similarweb reported last year on the devastating impact of AI Overviews on news publisher traffic — users see the answer directly on Google and have no incentive to click through to the original. Cloudflare introduced new policies and tools on July 1 allowing publishers to block AI crawlers or charge AI companies for content access through its marketplace, essentially arming content creators to fight back.

But the data paints a complex picture. While AI answers reduce direct click-through rates, the proportion of AI responses that include citation links has grown more than 5x over the past year. In other words, AI is increasingly citing sources in its responses. This offers a glimmer of hope for publishers — if AI answers can drive targeted, high-intent traffic, even if total volume is lower, the quality might be higher.

However, absolute citation rates remain low. As of May 2026, only 6.8% of U.S. desktop ChatGPT queries included citations (travel, retail, and sports fare slightly better). Google doesn't publish its own citation rate data, but Similarweb's monitoring suggests Google's AI Overviews cite more than ChatGPT but still far below 100%.

One positive signal: after a May 7 search update, ChatGPT's referral rate to websites doubled from 25% in March 2026 to nearly 60% by late May. This shows that when blue links are more prominent in AI search results, users do click — people don't refuse to read originals; AI interfaces were just burying them too deep.

New Survival Rules for Content Creators

Facing search's transformation from gateway to destination, content creators need to rethink their positioning.

If your content only answers factual questions ("what is X," "what are the steps for X"), AI Overviews will directly replace you — because AI can give the same answer faster and more conveniently. But if your content offers unique perspectives, deep analysis, first-hand experience, or a sense of community — these are things AI can't generate, and things AI answers will cite but can never replace.

In other words, the "information provider" role is depreciating, while the value of "perspective shapers" and "experience builders" is rising. This isn't the end of content — it's content upgrading, though the upgrade direction is toward harder, more unique, more human territory.

When search no longer requires clicking, the content that makes people actively seek it out is the content that truly matters.
