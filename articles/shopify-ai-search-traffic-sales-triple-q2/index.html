Shopify Q2 AI Search Drives Growth
Traffic and Orders Triple Year-Over-Year

与媒体网站被AI搜索抢流量不同，Shopify的AI搜索反而创造了新的购物入口——AI不是流量黑洞，而是增量引擎。

Unlike media sites losing traffic to AI search, Shopify's AI search is creating new shopping entry points — AI isn't a traffic black hole, it's a growth engine.

No.031 2026.08.07 约 4 分钟阅读 ~4 min read

AI搜索到底是在抢流量还是在创造流量？Shopify交出了一份很有意思的答卷。

根据Shopify 2026年Q2财报，平台上AI驱动的流量和订单同比增长了3倍（tripled year over year）。Shopify总裁Harley Finkelstein的原话很直接："AI没有像蚕食媒体网站那样蚕食搜索流量，反而为商家创造了全新的购物方式和发现渠道。"

这句话很重要。因为过去一年，关于AI搜索的主流叙事是悲观的：ChatGPT之类的AI助手会直接给用户答案，用户不再需要点击搜索结果，媒体网站的流量会被AI吃掉。确实，很多内容网站的SEO流量已经出现了明显下滑。

但电商是反过来的。AI搜索不但没有抢电商的流量，反而带来了更多的流量和订单。为什么？因为购物和获取信息是两种完全不同的行为模式。

为什么AI搜索对电商是增量而非减量

你搜一个问题，AI直接给你答案了，你就不会点进去看原文了——这是媒体的困境。因为信息获取这个动作，在AI那里就完成了闭环。

但购物不一样。你搜"夏天穿什么连衣裙好看"，AI给你推荐了几款，你会不会点进去看？当然会。你要看详情、看图片、看评价、看尺码、看能不能凑单。AI推荐的不是答案，是选项。选项越多，你点击和购买的概率就越高。

这就是AI搜索对电商的核心价值：它降低了发现的门槛。以前你要找一件合适的衣服，得搜关键词、翻很多页、一个个点开看。现在你用自然语言描述你想要的，AI就能帮你筛选出最匹配的选项。用户找东西更容易了，逛的时间更长了，下单的概率自然就更高了。

更关键的是，AI搜索能理解那些传统搜索引擎理解不了的模糊需求。"我下周要参加一个婚礼，想穿一件不太正式但又好看的裙子"——这种需求，传统关键词搜索根本处理不了，但AI可以。而这些"长尾的、模糊的、个性化的"需求，恰恰是转化率最高的需求——因为用户已经有明确的购买意图了，只是不知道具体买什么。

所以，AI搜索对于电商来说，不是抢存量，而是开增量。它把那些以前搜不到、找不到、无法表达的需求给挖掘出来了，创造了新的购物入口。

电商是AI落地的黄金赛道

Shopify的数据进一步印证了一个判断：电商可能是AI落地最快、ROI最清晰的垂直领域之一。

为什么这么说？因为电商的价值链条很短，效果很容易量化。用了AI之后，流量涨了多少？转化率提了多少？客单价增了多少？退货率降了多少？这些都是可以精确计算的数字。只要AI带来的收入增长大于AI的成本，商家就愿意掏钱。

这跟很多其他AI应用场景形成了鲜明对比。比如AI写文案，效果好不好很难量化；AI做客服，用户满意度提升了多少也不好精确衡量。但电商不一样——每一分钱的投入，都能算出几分钱的回报。

而且电商的付费意愿极强。对于商家来说，只要能带来更多订单，花多少钱都愿意——因为这不是成本，这是投资，有回报的。这也是为什么AI电商工具的付费转化特别好做。

当然，AI电商也不是没有挑战。比如推荐的准确性、个性化程度、退货率控制、假货问题……这些都需要时间去优化。但大方向是明确的：AI正在重构电商的人货场，从搜索到推荐、从客服到运营、从供应链到营销，每一个环节都在被AI重塑。

Shopify的3倍增长，只是一个开始。未来几年，AI对电商的改造会越来越深入。那些率先用好AI的商家，会获得巨大的竞争优势；而那些反应慢的，可能会被甩得越来越远。

明天见。

Is AI search stealing traffic or creating it? Shopify has delivered a very interesting answer.

According to Shopify's Q2 2026 earnings, AI-driven traffic and orders tripled year over year on the platform. Shopify president Harley Finkelstein put it directly: "AI isn't cannibalizing search traffic the way it has for media sites — instead, it's creating entirely new ways to shop and discover products for merchants."

That sentence matters. Because over the past year, the dominant narrative around AI search has been pessimistic: AI assistants like ChatGPT give users answers directly, users no longer need to click through to search results, and media sites lose traffic to AI. Indeed, many content sites have already seen significant declines in SEO traffic.

But e-commerce is the opposite. AI search hasn't taken traffic from e-commerce — it's brought more traffic and more orders. Why? Because shopping and information-seeking are two completely different behavioral patterns.

Why AI Search Is Incremental for E-commerce, Not Cannibalistic

You search for a question, AI gives you the answer directly, you don't click through to read the original — that's the media dilemma. Because the act of information acquisition closes the loop right there with the AI.

But shopping is different. You search "what dresses look good for summer," AI recommends a few options — will you click through? Of course you will. You want to see details, pictures, reviews, sizing, whether you can bundle items. AI doesn't recommend answers — it recommends options. The more options, the higher your chance of clicking and buying.

This is the core value of AI search for e-commerce: it lowers the barrier to discovery. Before, to find a suitable piece of clothing, you had to search keywords, flip through pages, click into each one. Now you describe what you want in natural language, and AI helps you filter the best matches. Users find things more easily, browse longer, and naturally have a higher chance of placing orders.

More critically, AI search can understand vague needs that traditional search engines can't. "I have a wedding next week, I want a dress that's not too formal but still looks good" — this kind of query, traditional keyword search simply can't handle, but AI can. And these "long-tail, vague, personalized" needs are precisely the ones with the highest conversion rates — because the user already has clear purchase intent, they just don't know exactly what to buy.

So for e-commerce, AI search isn't about stealing existing share — it's about opening up new increments. It digs out those previously unsearchable, unfindable, inexpressible needs, creating new shopping entry points.

E-commerce Is AI's Golden Vertical for Deployment

Shopify's data further confirms a judgment: e-commerce might be one of the verticals where AI deploys fastest and has the clearest ROI.

Why? Because e-commerce's value chain is short, and results are easily quantified. After using AI, how much did traffic go up? How much did conversion rate improve? How much did average order value increase? How much did return rates drop? These are all precisely calculable numbers. As long as the revenue growth from AI is greater than the cost of AI, merchants are willing to pay.

This stands in sharp contrast to many other AI application scenarios. Take AI copywriting, for example — it's hard to quantify how good the effect is. AI customer service — it's hard to precisely measure how much user satisfaction improved. But e-commerce is different — every cent invested can be calculated in terms of return.

And e-commerce has extremely high willingness to pay. For merchants, as long as it brings more orders, they'll spend whatever it takes — because this isn't cost, it's investment, with returns. That's also why AI e-commerce tools have such good paid conversion rates.

Of course, AI e-commerce isn't without challenges. Recommendation accuracy, personalization depth, return rate control, counterfeit issues — these all take time to optimize. But the big direction is clear: AI is restructuring e-commerce's people, goods, and places — from search to recommendation, from customer service to operations, from supply chain to marketing — every link is being reshaped by AI.

Shopify's tripled growth is just the beginning. Over the next few years, AI's transformation of e-commerce will go deeper and deeper. Merchants who get good at AI first will gain enormous competitive advantage; those who react slowly might get left further and further behind.
