Putting Agents in the CEO's Decision Chair

A team cut in an "AI transformation" went big: OpenExecutive hands CEO-level decisions to agents, and developers can't stop sharing the repo this week.

No.044 2026.08.27 约 5 分钟阅读 ~5 min read

今天的槽点，献给一间了不起的开源仓库：OpenExecutive——一个让 AI agent 承担 CEO 及高管职能的组织决策工具，作者是一支自称在公司“AI 转型”中被裁员的开发者团队。故事起点颇具行为艺术气质：你们用 AI 腾出我的工位，我就亲手把你们的会议室也做成可自动化模块。

项目本周在开发者圈持续发酵（星标与 fork 数字一天一变，咱就不引精确快照了，反正足够热闹），科技博客跟进一片，评论区吵出了三大流派。

流派一：黑色幽默派

他们指出一个微妙的对称性：当老板宣布“你的岗位可以被 AI 替代”时，默认自己的岗位自带不可替代性光环。而事实上，高管的日常工作——定 OKR、审预算、写汇报、画战略蓝图——恰恰是公司里最像 LLM 输出的文本活动。评论区有人补刀：“OpenExecutive 的第一版需求文档，就是把董事会纪要转成了 prompt。”

流派二：认真当真派

这一派已经在读架构了：把决策权限、否决规则、责任背书机制建模进 agent 工作流，理论上确实可行。问题从来不在技术能不能模拟决策，而在决策出错时谁负责。AI 可以替你签掉一张报销单，暂时没法替你被员工堵在电梯里骂半小时。

流派三：照镜子派

也是本栏目最共情的一派：这个项目的价值根本不是复仇文学，而是一面递给管理层和打工人的双向镜子。打工人看见自己的工种有多脆弱，老板看见自己的核心职能有多少其实是流程皮肤。自动化你的工作叫降本增效，自动化他的决策就叫行为艺术——这不合理。

仓库还在活跃更新，有几个模块做得意外地像回事。指望它明天就把 CEO 选下去并不现实；但拿它反向审计一家公司的决策链条冗余度，说不定明年就会出现在某咨询公司的提案模板里。

Today's Cao goes to a magnificent repository: OpenExecutive — an organizational decision tool that hands CEO and executive functions to AI agents, authored by a dev team that says it was cut loose during the company's own “AI transformation.” The origin story has real performance-art energy: you used AI to clear out my workstation, so I'll turn your boardroom into an automatable module.

The project has been fermenting among developers all week (stars and forks move daily, so no precise snapshot counts here — let's just say lively), with tech blogs piling in and comment sections splitting into three schools.

School One: Black Humor

They point out a delicious symmetry: when the boss announces “your role can be replaced by AI,” his own role arrives pre-crowned with irreplaceability. Yet executive daily work — OKRs, budget reviews, status decks, strategy blueprints — is precisely the most LLM-shaped textual activity in any company. One commenter landed the dagger: “OpenExecutive's v1 requirements doc was just board minutes converted into prompts.”

School Two: Take It Seriously

This crowd is already reading the architecture: modeling decision authority, veto rules, and accountability endorsements into agent workflows is theoretically feasible. The question was never whether tech can simulate a decision — it's who takes the blame when one goes wrong. AI can sign off an expense report for you; it cannot yet stand in the elevator getting scolded by employees for half an hour.

School Three: The Mirror

And the school this column relates to most: the project's value isn't revenge literature but a two-way mirror handed to both management and workers. Workers see how fragile their job descriptions are; bosses see how much of their core function is process skin. Automating your work is called cost efficiency; automating his decision-making is called performance art — that doesn't add up.

The repo is still under active commits, some modules surprisingly solid. Expecting it to vote the CEO out tomorrow isn't realistic; using it to reverse-audit a company's decision-chain redundancy? That could be in some consultancy's proposal deck next year.

实用提醒：1. 面对“AI转型”“组织优化”类裁员沟通，先拿到书面方案再谈条件，口头承诺不作数；2. 把自己的核心工作流整理成文档并保留底稿——它既是交接证据也是面试素材；3. 与其抱怨不如先武装自己：今天这个仓库的架构思路值得亲手搭一遍。
Practical tips: 1. For “AI transformation” or “reorg” layoff conversations, get the plan in writing before negotiating — verbal promises don't count; 2. Document your core workflow and keep your own copy — it doubles as handover evidence and interview material; 3. Arm yourself instead of complaining: today's repo architecture is worth building once with your own hands.
