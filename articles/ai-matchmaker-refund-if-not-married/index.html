前Kimi搜索负责人做AI红娘 承诺三年不结婚全额退款

Former Kimi Search Head Launches AI Matchmaker Full Refund If You're Not Married in 3 Years

"拉高10%结婚率""三年不结婚就退款"——当AI开始搞婚恋，这承诺比任何情人节告白都勇。

"Raise marriage rates by 10%" "full refund if not married in three years" — when AI gets into matchmaking, the promises are bolder than any Valentine's Day confession.

No.042 2026.08.24 约 4 分钟阅读 ~4 min read

朋友们，AI创业的卷度，已经卷到婚恋市场了。

这周AI圈最有梗的创业项目，不是又一个Agent框架，不是又一个视频生成模型——是一个"AI红娘"。创始人背景很硬核：前Kimi搜索负责人。目标很宏大：拉高10%的结婚率。承诺很勇猛：用了三年还没结婚，全额退款。

你品，你细品。"三年不结婚就退款"——这比任何婚恋平台的会员承诺都狠。世纪佳缘、百合网不敢说的话，AI红娘敢说；你妈逼你相亲时不敢拍的胸脯，AI敢拍。

这事儿为什么这么Cao？

首先，这个承诺本身就充满了荒诞感。你买个AI编程工具，它说"用三年写不出10万行代码退款"——这还能量化。你买个AI红娘，它说"三年不结婚退款"——这怎么算？是AI给你介绍的人你没看上算谁的？是你看上了人家没看上你算谁的？是谈恋爱谈了两年半分手了算谁的？是你结婚了三个月又离了算不算KPI达标？

这里面的变量太多了，多到可以养活十个法学博士写论文。但人家就是敢承诺，这就是AI时代的创业勇气——反正先把牛吹出去，三年后还不知道这家公司在不在呢。

其次，"拉高10%结婚率"这个目标也很有意思。现在全国结婚率年年下降，年轻人不谈恋爱不结婚已经成了社会难题。社会学家研究了几十年没解决的问题，一个AI产品说要拉高10%——这勇气，比梁静茹给的还多。

AI能做什么？给你推荐更"匹配"的人？帮你写聊天开场白？帮你分析约会对象的潜台词？这些确实能提高效率，但能不能走到结婚这一步，变量太多了——房子、车子、彩礼、婆媳关系、三观一致……哪一个是AI能解决的？

当然，我们也不能一棒子打死。AI确实在信息匹配层面有优势，它能比传统红娘更高效地筛选条件、分析偏好、推荐人选。但婚姻这件事，从来就不是一个"匹配效率"问题——它是一个"感觉"问题、一个"时机"问题、一个"运气"问题。这些，AI暂时还搞不定。

创业启示录

这个项目虽然Cao，但它背后折射出的AI创业趋势很有意思：AI正在从To B、To Developer的工具，快速向各种垂直消费场景渗透。婚恋、教育、医疗、法律、心理咨询……每一个"信息不对称+决策成本高"的领域，都有人在用AI重做一遍。

至于最后能不能成，那是另一回事。但这种"敢承诺、敢吹牛、敢All in"的劲头，确实是AI创业这个阶段特有的景观——反正算力便宜，反正API方便，先做出来再说，万一成了呢？

给朋友们几个实用提醒：

第一，AI推荐的对象，还是要自己见真人。AI看数据不看气场，匹配度99%的人，见面可能5分钟就想跑。

第二，任何承诺"包结果"的服务，都要打个问号。尤其是感情这种事，没人能给你打包票——AI不行，月老也不行。

第三，结婚不是KPI。别因为一个AI产品给你画饼，就急着把自己嫁出去/娶进来。缘分到了自然成，缘分不到，AI也救不了你。

今天就槽到这里，明天继续。

Friends, AI startup grind has officially reached the matchmaking market.

The most meme-worthy AI startup this week isn't another agent framework or video generation model — it's an "AI matchmaker." The founder has a serious background: former head of search at Kimi. The goal is ambitious: raise marriage rates by 10%. The promise is ballsy: full refund if you're not married after three years of use.

Let that sink in. "Full refund if not married in three years" — that's a guarantee even Jiayuan and Baihe, China's biggest dating platforms, would never dare make. Your mom nagging you to get married doesn't have the guts to promise that. An AI matchmaker does.

Why Is This So Cao?

First, the promise itself is pure absurdity. If you buy an AI coding tool and it says "refund if you don't write 100K lines of code in three years," that's at least quantifiable. But an AI matchmaker saying "refund if not married in three years" — how do you measure that? If AI sets you up and you don't like them, whose fault is that? If you like them and they reject you? If you date for two and a half years and break up? If you get married and divorce three months later — does that count as hitting the KPI?

There are enough variables here to keep ten law PhDs busy writing dissertations. But they made the promise anyway. That's AI-era startup courage — make the bold claim first, who knows if the company will even exist in three years.

Second, the "raise marriage rates by 10%" target is something else. Marriage rates in China have been declining for years; young people not dating or getting married is a full-blown social crisis. Sociologists have studied this for decades without solving it, and an AI product says it'll move the needle by 10% — that's more confidence than a motivational speaker on TikTok.

What can AI actually do? Recommend more "compatible" people? Write your opening chat lines? Analyze your date's subtext? That improves efficiency, sure. But getting to marriage involves way too many variables — housing, cars, bride price, in-law dynamics, value alignment… none of which AI can solve.

Of course, we shouldn't write it off entirely. AI does have an edge in information matching — it can filter criteria, analyze preferences, and recommend candidates more efficiently than traditional matchmakers. But marriage was never a "matching efficiency" problem — it's a "chemistry" problem, a "timing" problem, a "luck" problem. Those, AI can't fix yet.

Startup Lessons

While the project is meme-worthy, the trend it reflects is interesting: AI is rapidly expanding from B2B and developer tools into every vertical consumer scenario. Matchmaking, education, healthcare, legal, therapy… every field with "information asymmetry + high decision costs" is getting redone with AI.

Whether any of these actually work is another question. But that "make bold promises, ship fast, swing for the fences" energy is uniquely characteristic of this AI startup moment — compute is cheap, APIs are easy, build it first and see what sticks.

A few practical reminders for y'all:

First, always meet the person in real life, no matter what AI says. AI reads data, not chemistry. A 99% match might make you want to run for the exit after five minutes IRL.

Second, any service promising "guaranteed results" deserves a big question mark. Especially with matters of the heart — nobody can guarantee you love. Not AI, not your mom, not even Yue Lao (the Chinese god of marriage).

Third, marriage isn't a KPI. Don't rush to marry yourself off because an AI product made big promises. When it's right, it's right. When it's not, AI can't save you.

That's all the cao for today. See you tomorrow.
