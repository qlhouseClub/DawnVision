Friend AI吊坠重出江湖
加个喇叭价格翻倍，融250万花180万买域名的行为艺术还在继续

Friend Pendant Relaunches with Speaker
Doubles Price, $1.8M Domain Saga Continues

曾众筹200万美元的Friend AI吊坠发布2.0版，加了内置喇叭价格从$129涨到$249，9个月前的纽约地铁抗议恍如隔日。

The $2M Kickstarter Friend AI pendant launches 2.0 with a built-in speaker, price jumps from $129 to $249 — nine months after the NYC subway protest.

No.026 2026.07.31 约 4 分钟阅读 ~4 min read

还记得2025年初那个刷爆科技媒体的Friend AI吊坠吗？就是那个号称"戴在脖子上、24小时陪你说话的AI朋友"、在Kickstarter上众筹了200万美元、让无数人觉得"这就是AI硬件的未来"的小东西？

它回来了——不过是以一种让你哭笑不得的方式。

7月31日，Friend团队发布了新版本。加了个喇叭，价格从$129涨到了$249。加了个喇叭。涨了一倍。

对，就加了个喇叭。

那些年，Friend烧过的钱

我们来回顾一下这家"AI陪伴硬件"创业公司的辉煌战绩：

1. 180万美元买域名。融了250万美元，其中180万用来收购friend.com域名。也就是说，融资的72%花在了一个网址上。剩下的钱够做产品吗？从结果来看是不够的。

2. 纽约地铁铺天盖地打广告。整个纽约地铁都被Friend的广告覆盖了，"Always by your side"的标语配上一个小小的吊坠，看起来温情脉脉。然后纽约人民怒了——我们已经够孤独了，你还来卖AI朋友？于是爆发了大规模抗议。

3. 创始人亲自参加抗议自己产品的活动。Avi Schiffmann，这位当年做疫情追踪网站一举成名的创业者，发推宣传抗议活动，还亲自到场。抗议自己公司的产品？这波操作属于是NFT自毁艺术的硬件版了。

"融250万、180万买域名、地铁打广告、被抗议、创始人去抗议现场、沉寂9个月、加个喇叭价格翻倍重出江湖。这不是产品迭代，这是行为艺术。"—— Dawn Vision编辑部

加个喇叭值$120吗

新版本新增的功能：内置喇叭，可以语音对话（原来只能文字回复）。新增的收费：$10/月订阅费，用于保存超过30天的对话记忆。

加个喇叭成本多少？根据供应链估算，一个体积这么小的吊坠用的微型扬声器，成本大概是2-3美元。然后零售价涨了120美元。40倍溢价。

这让我想起了一个古老的商业寓言：一个人去修水管，敲了一下收100美元。人家说敲一下凭什么收100美元？他说敲一下值1美元，知道往哪敲值99美元。

Friend版本的寓言是：加个喇叭成本3美元，知道加喇叭能卖贵120美元——值117美元。

给AI硬件创业者的三个实用提醒

笑归笑，Friend的案例还是有借鉴意义的：

1. 先在手机App里验证需求，再做硬件。如果AI伴侣在App里留不住用户，做成吊坠也留不住。

2. 交付比概念重要100倍。一个按时发货的平庸产品，胜过永远在"即将发布"的革命性产品。

3. 对硬件怀有敬畏心。苹果做AirPods花了三年，做Vision Pro花了七年。你十几人的团队凭什么18个月做出革命产品？

最后说一句：如果你想做AI硬件，请先想清楚用户买的到底是什么。是功能？是陪伴？是社交货币？还是地铁广告创造的FOMO？

想清楚了再做。

今天就槽到这里，明天继续。

Remember the Friend AI pendant that blew up in early 2025? The "AI friend you wear around your neck" that raised $2 million on Kickstarter and made everyone think "this is the future of AI hardware"?

It's back — in a way that makes you facepalm.

On July 31, Friend released a new version. They added a speaker, and the price went from $129 to $249. Added a speaker. Doubled the price.

Yep. Just a speaker.

The Money Friend Burned

Let's recap this "AI companion hardware" startup's greatest hits:

1. $1.8M on a domain name. Raised $2.5M, spent $1.8M acquiring friend.com. 72% of funding on a URL. Was there enough left for the actual product? Judging by results — no.

2. Blanketed NYC subway with ads. "Always by your side" with a tiny pendant, all over the subway. Then New Yorkers got mad — we're already lonely enough, you're selling us AI friends? Mass protests ensued.

3. Founder personally attended the protest against his own product. Avi Schiffmann — who made his name building COVID tracking sites — tweeted promoting the protest and showed up in person. Protesting your own product? That's the hardware version of NFT self-destruct art.

"Raised $2.5M, spent $1.8M on a domain, subway ads, got protested, founder joined the protest, 9 months of silence, then added a speaker and doubled the price. This isn't product iteration — it's performance art."—— The Dawn Vision Editorial Desk

Is a Speaker Worth $120?

New feature: built-in speaker for voice conversations (previously text-only). New charge: $10/month subscription for memory beyond 30 days.

How much does a speaker like that cost? Supply chain estimates put a micro-speaker of this size at $2-3. Retail price went up $120. That's a 40x markup.

Reminds me of the old parable: a guy fixes a pipe, charges $100 for one tap. "Why $100 for one tap?" "The tap is $1. Knowing where to tap is $99."

Friend version: the speaker is $3. Knowing adding a speaker lets you charge $120 more — that's $117.

Three Practical Reminders for AI Hardware Founders

Jokes aside, the Friend case is instructive:

1. Validate in an app before building hardware. If an AI companion can't retain users in an app, it won't retain them as a pendant either.

2. Delivery matters 100x more than the concept. A mediocre product that ships on time beats a revolutionary product that's always "coming soon."

3. Have respect for hardware. Apple spent 3 years on AirPods, 7 on Vision Pro. Why does your 15-person team think you can ship a revolution in 18 months?

Final thought: if you're building AI hardware, figure out what users are actually buying. Functionality? Companionship? Social currency? Or FOMO created by subway ads?

Figure it out first.
