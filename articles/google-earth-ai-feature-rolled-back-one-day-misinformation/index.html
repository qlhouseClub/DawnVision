朋友们，今天的槽点故事，主角又是Google——没错，那个声称"不作恶"（Don't be evil）的Google。

事情是这样的：Google最近给Google地球（Google Earth）加了个AI功能——能在真实的卫星地图上，直接生成AI画面并叠加进去。听起来很酷是不是？你可以给你家小区加个游泳池，给公司门口放个巨型雕塑，纯娱乐嘛。

然而，功能上线不到24小时，Google就紧急把它回滚了。为什么？因为有个叫Henk van Ess的调查记者，用这个功能在真实地图上生成了：

——一座不存在的核电站（边境地带）

——一个不存在的难民营（争议地区）

——一场不存在的致命车祸（阿姆斯特丹街头）

——一个不存在的弹坑（战地医院旁边）

BBC Verify也不甘寂寞，生成了倒塌的埃菲尔铁塔。

你品，你细品。这些东西放在真实的卫星地图上，真假难辨——尤其是对那些不懂AI生成、分不清AI和真实卫星图的人来说，这就是"有图有真相"的铁证啊。

10亿人的地图，变成谣言工厂需要几步？

Google地球和Google地图的用户有多少？超过10亿。基本上你能想到的人，都用过Google地图。这是全球最权威、最广泛使用的地理信息平台，没有之一。

你想想这个画面：某国大选前夕，一张"卫星图"在社交平台疯传，显示某候选人的豪宅里藏着直升机和金条。转发的人说"你自己去Google地球上看"——等等，现在Google地球上的画面，可能是某个网友生成的啊！

或者更极端的：两个国家局势紧张的时候，一张"卫星图"显示对方在边境部署了导弹。你说这会不会引发误判？会不会有人当真？

Google的产品经理可能觉得："哎呀大家就是玩玩嘛，谁会当真呢？"——典型的硅谷理想主义思维。他们假设用户都是善良的、都是来娱乐的，不会有人恶意使用。但现实是：只要工具存在，就一定会有人用它做坏事。而且越是权威的平台，被恶意使用后的破坏力就越大。

最讽刺的是什么？Google前几年还在搞各种打击谣言、打击虚假信息的项目，结果自己直接把AI造谣工具给做到了全球最权威的地图平台上。这波操作，属实是左手中右手互搏了。

AI时代，我们还能相信什么？

说真的，Google Earth这件事只是个开始。以后AI生成的内容会越来越多、越来越真、越来越难分辨。

以前你看到一张照片，还可以想想"PS的吧？"以后呢？AI生成的卫星图、AI生成的视频、AI生成的语音、AI生成的文档——每一种信息载体都可能被AI伪造，而且伪造的成本几乎为零。

那怎么办？总不能什么都不信吧。

说实话，没有完美的解决方案。但有几个简单的习惯，可以帮你避开90%的坑：

第一，看到"大新闻"先别急着转。尤其是那种让你特别愤怒、特别震惊的内容，先缓一缓。真正的大新闻，不差你转发的那几分钟。等几个小时，看看有没有权威媒体报道、有没有官方回应。

第二，交叉验证。一张图说是在某地，你去其他地图平台看看同一个位置是不是一样的；一个消息说是某媒体报道的，你去搜搜那个媒体的官网有没有。一个来源可能有问题，但三五个独立来源都指向同一个事实，可信度就高多了。

第三，相信官方信源，但也要打个折。政府公告、公司官网、上市公司财报——这些是相对可信的。但也要注意：官方说的不一定就是全部真相，它可能只是选择性地告诉你一部分。所以即使是官方消息，也要结合上下文和常识来判断。

AI让信息的生产变得无比廉价，也让辨别信息的成本变得无比高昂。以前说"眼见为实"，现在眼见也不一定为实了。在这个真假难辨的时代，保持怀疑精神，可能比掌握任何AI工具都重要。

今天就槽到这里，明天继续。

Friends, today's Cao! story stars Google again — yes, the Google that used to say "Don't be evil."

Here's what happened: Google recently added an AI feature to Google Earth — it lets you generate AI imagery and overlay it directly on real satellite maps. Sounds cool, right? You could add a swimming pool to your neighborhood, put a giant sculpture in front of your office, just for fun.

However, less than 24 hours after the feature launched, Google urgently rolled it back. Why? Because an investigative journalist named Henk van Ess used the feature to generate on real maps:

— A nonexistent nuclear power plant (in a border region)

— A nonexistent refugee camp (in a disputed area)

— A nonexistent fatal car crash (on the streets of Amsterdam)

— A nonexistent bomb crater (next to a field hospital)

Not to be outdone, BBC Verify generated a collapsed Eiffel Tower.

Let that sink in. These things placed on real satellite maps are indistinguishable from reality — especially for people who don't understand AI generation and can't tell AI apart from real satellite imagery. To them, this is "picture proof" — ironclad evidence.

How Many Steps to Turn a 1B-User Map Into a Disinformation Factory?

How many users do Google Earth and Google Maps have? Over 1 billion. Basically, everyone you can think of has used Google Maps. It's the most authoritative, most widely used geographic information platform on the planet — bar none.

Imagine this scenario: right before a national election, a "satellite image" goes viral on social media showing a candidate's mansion hiding helicopters and gold bars. People sharing it say "go check it yourself on Google Earth" — except, wait, the imagery on Google Earth now could have been generated by some random internet user!

Or something more extreme: when tensions are high between two countries, a "satellite image" shows the other side deploying missiles at the border. Do you think that could cause a miscalculation? Do you think someone might take it seriously?

Google's product managers probably thought: "C'mon, people are just playing around — who would take it seriously?" — classic Silicon Valley idealism. They assume all users are well-intentioned, all here for entertainment, no one would use it maliciously. But the reality is: if a tool exists, someone will use it for bad things. And the more authoritative the platform, the greater the damage when it's misused.

The most ironic part? Google has spent recent years on all sorts of projects fighting disinformation and fake news — and then they go and build an AI disinformation tool directly into the world's most authoritative mapping platform. That move is basically left hand fighting right hand.

In the AI Era, What Can We Still Trust?

Honestly, the Google Earth incident is just the beginning. From here on out, AI-generated content will keep getting more, more realistic, and harder to distinguish.

Before, when you saw a photo, you could wonder "is it Photoshopped?" What about from now on? AI-generated satellite imagery, AI-generated video, AI-generated voice, AI-generated documents — every type of information载体 can be faked by AI, and the cost of faking it is practically zero.

So what do we do? We can't just disbelieve everything, right?

To be honest, there's no perfect solution. But a few simple habits can help you avoid 90% of the traps:

First, don't rush to share "breaking news." Especially content that makes you especially angry or shocked — take a moment. Real big news won't suffer from you waiting a few minutes to repost. Wait a few hours, see if authoritative media cover it, see if there's an official response.

Second, cross-verify. If an image says something happened at a certain location, check other map platforms to see if the same location matches. If a story says it was reported by some media outlet, search that outlet's official site to see if it's there. One source might be problematic, but when three to five independent sources all point to the same facts, credibility is much higher.

Third, trust official sources, but with a grain of salt. Government announcements, company websites, public company financial reports — these are relatively credible. But note: what officials say isn't necessarily the whole truth; they might just be selectively telling you part of it. So even with official news, judge it in context and with common sense.

AI has made information production incredibly cheap, and made the cost of辨别 information incredibly expensive. We used to say "seeing is believing" — now seeing doesn't necessarily mean believing. In an era where truth and falsehood are hard to tell apart, maintaining a skeptical spirit might be more important than掌握 any AI tool.
