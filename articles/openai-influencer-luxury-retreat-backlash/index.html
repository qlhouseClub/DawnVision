2000美元一晚采蜂蜜被骂虚伪

OpenAI Luxury Farm Influencer Event Backfires
$2,000/Night Honey Harvest Draws "Hypocrisy" Claims

OpenAI在纽约2000美元一晚的豪华度假村请网红采蜂蜜推广ChatGPT Work，网友骂声一片。一边是"AI造福全人类"的宏大叙事，一边是农场疗愈奢华派对，反差感拉满。

OpenAI invited influencers to a $2,000/night luxury resort in New York to harvest honey and promote ChatGPT Work, drawing widespread backlash. Grand narratives of "AI benefiting all humanity" on one side, farm-therapy luxury parties on the other — the irony could not be thicker.

No.028 2026.08.04 约 4 分钟阅读 ~4 min read

朋友们，今天的AI圈乐子，简直可以写进《科技公司公关反面教材大全》的第一章。

事情是这样的：OpenAI最近在纽约上州的哈德逊河谷搞了个"创作者夏令营"，地点选在一个豪华度假村，周末房价每晚超过2000美元，差不多一万三人民币。请的不是工程师，不是研究人员，是Instagram和TikTok上的网红——生活方式博主、职场博主、个人成长博主那种。

活动内容是什么呢？农场疗愈，采蜂蜜，画画，田园牧歌。长桌宴尽头摆着OpenAI的大Logo闪闪发光，吹着哈德逊河谷的小风，大家一起采采蜂蜜聊聊天，再聊聊ChatGPT Work的使用体验，发发Ins故事，完美的品牌活动。

帖子一发，评论区炸了

OpenAI本来想的是：网红们Ins一发，粉丝们一看"哇OpenAI好有格调"，ChatGPT Work美美出圈，品牌形象高端大气上档次。

结果呢？评论区全是骂的。

"一边说AGI造福全人类，一边请网红住两千美元一晚的酒店采蜂蜜？这也太虚伪了吧。"

"你们咋不住到数据中心里去呢？俄亥俄州5000亿美元数据中心项目的环境影响报告写完了吗？"

"世界正处于水深火热之中，现在可不是炫耀豪华酒店的好时机。"

还有更扎心的："这些人都是谁啊？我一个做AI开发的用了你们产品三年，从来没被邀请过；一个在TikTok上教怎么用AI写简历的，被请去采蜂蜜了？"

Business Insider更损，直接给OpenAI发了三个问题：创作者有没有报酬？差旅住宿谁买单？有没有要求创作者必须发内容？OpenAI一个都没正面回答。据报道，已经有参与活动的网红偷偷删掉了相关视频——本来想蹭个高大上的活动，结果被骂成这样，不值当。

Anthropic和微软也干过，为啥只有OpenAI被骂

有人可能会说：科技公司请创作者搞活动不是很正常吗？Anthropic给Claude办过创作者晚宴，微软Copilot请过网红去超级碗品牌旅行，怎么到OpenAI这儿就翻车了？

因为吃相和人设的反差太大了。OpenAI的官方叙事是什么？"我们是一家以使命为导向的公司，我们的目标是确保AGI造福全人类，我们是非营利起家（虽然现在不是了），我们在做拯救世界级别的重要工作。"然后你转头就花几百万美元请生活方式网红住豪华农场采蜂蜜？

这就像一个天天在朋友圈发"我要改变世界"的创业者，你转头在五星级酒店泳池边碰到他开香槟——你也不会觉得他十恶不赦，但你肯定会觉得：兄弟，你这人设是不是有点裂了？

而且时机也不对。最近OpenAI刚因为俄亥俄州数据中心的环境问题被骂，Astra数学证明发布时还在讲"AI为人类探索知识前沿"，结果没几天就搞出这么一出。你哪怕等一个月再办呢？

给AI公司公关提个醒

最后给所有AI公司的公关团队提几个不成熟的小建议。

第一，搞活动之前先想想你的公司人设是什么。你是一家"改变世界"的使命驱动公司，还是一家卖软件的普通科技公司？如果是前者，低调一点没坏处；如果是后者，那你请多少网红都没人管你。

第二，网红营销不是万能药。B端产品请C端网红推广，效果本来就存疑；再加上这种奢华排场，除了让你的核心用户（开发者、企业客户）觉得被冒犯，没什么好处。

第三，采蜂蜜这个点子到底是谁想出来的啊？

今天就槽到这里，明天继续。

Friends, today's AI circus could be Chapter 1 of the textbook "Tech PR Fails: A Complete Anthology."

Here's what went down: OpenAI recently threw a "creator summer camp" in New York's Hudson Valley, at a luxury resort where weekend rates run over $2,000 a night — roughly ¥13,000 RMB. The guests weren't engineers or researchers. They were Instagram and TikTok influencers — lifestyle creators, career gurus, personal growth coaches.

The activities? Farm therapy, honey harvesting, painting, pastoral bliss. A long banquet table with a glowing OpenAI logo at the end, Hudson Valley breezes, everyone harvesting honey and chatting about their ChatGPT Work experiences, posting Instagram Stories — the perfect brand event.

The Posts Went Up, and the Comments Section Exploded

What OpenAI expected: influencers post pretty pictures, fans see "wow OpenAI is so classy," ChatGPT Work gets a nice viral bump, premium brand image secured.

What happened? The comments were uniformly brutal.

"Talking about AGI benefiting all humanity while flying influencers to $2,000/night hotels to harvest honey? The hypocrisy is stunning."

"Why don't you go live in a data center? Finished that environmental impact report for the $500B Ohio data center project yet?"

"The world is on fire — this isn't the moment to show off luxury resorts."

The most stinging comment: "Who even are these people? I've been building on your API for three years and never got invited; someone on TikTok teaching people to write resumes with AI gets a honey-harvesting retreat?"

Business Insider piled on, sending OpenAI three questions: Were creators paid? Who covered travel and lodging? Were creators required to post? OpenAI didn't directly answer a single one. According to reports, several attending influencers have quietly deleted their posts about the event — they came for a classy brand trip and got ratioed into oblivion. Not worth it.

Anthropic and Microsoft Did It Too — Why Only OpenAI Got Roasted

You might be thinking: tech companies hosting creator events is normal. Anthropic threw creator dinners for Claude; Microsoft flew influencers to the Super Bowl for Copilot. Why did only OpenAI faceplant?

Because the gap between branding and behavior was a chasm. What's OpenAI's official narrative? "We're a mission-driven company, our goal is ensuring AGI benefits all humanity, we started as a nonprofit (even if we aren't one anymore), we're doing world-saving important work." And then you turn around and drop millions flying lifestyle influencers to a luxury farm to harvest honey?

It's like the founder posting every day on WeChat Moments about "changing the world," and then you run into him popping champagne by a five-star hotel pool — you don't think he's evil, but you definitely think: bro, is your persona cracking a little?

And the timing was terrible. OpenAI was just getting dragged over Ohio data center environmental concerns, Astra's math proof launch was still talking about "AI pushing humanity's knowledge frontier," and days later this drops. Couldn't you have waited a month?

A Few Notes for AI PR Teams

A few humble suggestions for all AI company PR teams out there.

One: before throwing an event, remember what your company persona is. Are you a "change the world" mission-driven company, or a regular software shop selling tools? If the former, keeping a low profile doesn't hurt. If the latter, fly as many influencers as you want — nobody cares.

Two: influencer marketing isn't a silver bullet. Promoting a B2B product with consumer-facing influencers is questionable at best; add the luxury optics, and you mainly manage to offend your actual core users (developers, enterprise customers).

Three: whose idea was honey harvesting, seriously?

That's enough ranting for today. More tomorrow.
