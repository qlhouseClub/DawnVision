从8月2日开始，如果你在欧盟境内和一个聊天机器人对话，它必须在第一句话就告诉你"我是AI"。

欧盟委员会7月31日宣布，自8月2日起，欧盟委员会人工智能办公室与27个成员国主管部门共同开始执行《人工智能法》相关规定。这是全球首部全面AI监管法规从"立法阶段"正式进入"执法阶段"的标志。

哪些条款今天生效？

首批强制执行的主要是透明度条款，直接影响普通用户日常接触到的AI产品：

第一，所有与用户直接交互的AI系统——聊天机器人、AI客服、虚拟助手、数字人——必须在第一次互动开始时明确告知用户"你正在与AI交互"。不能伪装成真人，不能在对话中刻意隐藏AI身份。

第二，利用AI生成或编辑的深度伪造图片、视频、音频内容必须进行标识，而且需要添加机器可识别标记，以便平台和监管机构自动识别和追踪。这意味着你在社交媒体上看到的AI生成内容，不只是加一个"AI生成"的水印那么简单——还需要嵌入技术标记，让机器也能检测出来。

第三，欧盟委员会当天公布了首批签署《AI生成内容透明度行为准则》的机构名单——180多家，包括科技公司、内容平台、行业组织。签署方承诺落实透明度要求，配合监管执行。

影响有多大？

欧盟AI法案2024年8月1日正式生效，采取分阶段实施策略。2024年生效后，禁止不可接受风险AI系统的条款率先适用；2025年部分高风险AI条款落地；2026年8月2日是大部分核心条款——尤其是透明度要求和通用AI规则——正式进入执法阶段的时间节点。

对中国企业来说，这意味着只要你的产品服务欧盟用户，就必须遵守透明度要求。跨境电商的AI客服、出海App的聊天功能、面向欧洲市场的AI内容工具，都需要在产品流程中加入AI身份披露和内容标识。不合规的产品可能面临最高全球营业额6%的罚款。

更深远的影响在行业标准层面。欧盟的AI监管一向有"布鲁塞尔效应"——欧盟制定的标准往往会成为全球事实标准。GDPR重塑了全球隐私合规的格局，欧盟AI法案很可能同样重塑AI透明度的全球标准。当27个发达国家的市场要求AI亮明身份，其他国家和平台大概率会跟进类似规则。

当然，执法层面的挑战也不小。AI生成内容的标识在技术上如何落地、机器可读标记的标准是什么、跨境AI服务如何监管——这些细节还需要后续执行中逐步明确。但8月2日这个日期本身已经写入历史：从这一天起，AI在全球最大的发达经济体之一里，不再是法外之地。

明天见。

Starting August 2, if you chat with a bot anywhere in the EU, it must tell you "I'm AI" in its very first message.

The European Commission announced on July 31 that beginning August 2, the EU AI Office and competent authorities across all 27 member states would jointly enforce provisions of the AI Act. This marks the world's first comprehensive AI regulation moving from "legislative phase" to "enforcement phase."

What Takes Effect Today?

The first wave of mandatory enforcement centers on transparency provisions, directly affecting AI products that everyday users encounter:

First, all AI systems that interact directly with users — chatbots, AI customer service, virtual assistants, digital humans — must clearly inform users at the start of first interaction that "you are interacting with AI." No impersonating humans, no deliberately hiding AI identity during conversation.

Second, AI-generated or edited deepfake images, videos, and audio must be labeled and carry machine-readable markers so platforms and regulators can automatically detect and trace them. That means AI-generated content on social media won't just get a visible "AI-generated" watermark — it needs embedded technical markers that machines can detect too.

Third, the Commission published the first batch of signatories to the Code of Practice on AI-generated content transparency — over 180 organizations including tech companies, content platforms, and industry groups, committing to implement transparency requirements and cooperate with enforcement.

How Big Is the Impact?

The EU AI Act officially entered into force on August 1, 2024, with a phased implementation schedule. After the 2024 ban on unacceptable-risk AI and 2025 high-risk AI provisions, August 2, 2026 is when most core provisions — especially transparency requirements and general-purpose AI rules — officially become enforceable.

For Chinese companies, this means any product serving EU users must comply with transparency rules. Cross-border e-commerce AI customer service, chat features in global apps, AI content tools targeting the European market — all need to add AI identity disclosure and content labeling into product flows. Non-compliant products face fines up to 6% of global turnover.

The deeper impact is at the industry-standard level. EU regulation has a well-documented "Brussels Effect" — EU standards tend to become global de facto standards. GDPR reshaped global privacy compliance; the EU AI Act will likely do the same for AI transparency. When a market of 27 developed economies requires AI to identify itself, other countries and platforms will likely follow with similar rules.

Enforcement challenges remain significant. How to technically implement AI content labeling, what machine-readable marker standards look like, how to regulate cross-border AI services — these details will be worked out through enforcement. But August 2 is already in the history books: from this day forward, AI is no longer lawless territory in one of the world's largest developed economies.
