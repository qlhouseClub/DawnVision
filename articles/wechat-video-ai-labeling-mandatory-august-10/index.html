AI带货强制标注今日生效
漏标限流，平台当起看门人

Mandatory AI Labeling for Commerce Today
Unlabeled Means Throttled

8月10日起，视频号带货场景AI生成内容必须显著标注：数字人口播、AI商品图、合成演示都要亮明身份，漏标直接限流。

From Aug 10, AI-generated content in WeChat Video commerce must be clearly labeled: digital humans, AI product images, synthetic demos all show their ID. Missing labels mean throttled distribution.

No.032 2026.08.10 约 5 分钟阅读 ~5 min read

做AI带货的商家，从今天开始必须"亮身份证"了。

8月10日，微信视频号一条新规正式生效：带货视频里只要用了AI——不管是一段数字人口播、一张AI生成的商品海报，还是AI合成的使用效果演示——都必须主动标注来源。漏标会怎样？限制分发，流量直接砍。

据界面新闻8月3日报道，视频号发布的《短视频带货场景AI应用规范》把AI标注从过去的"建议"变成了"强制"，列出四类必须标注的情形：AI生成的商品展示画面和使用效果、AI人物口播和数字人出镜、AI生成的商品海报和宣传图、其他AI生成的视频画面。

这一次，视频号干了一件以往平台没干过的事：把带货场景和普通场景切开了。

普通内容靠自觉，带货内容必须亮明身份

新规最精妙的设计，是区别对待。普通内容的AI创作以倡导标注为主，不标也不影响流量；但你的视频一旦挂上商品链接，AI标注就从道德义务变成了准入门槛。不标，限流；标了但内容与实物不符，照样限流。

这刀切得准。过去一年，AI生成视频的门槛低到什么程度？几分钟出一条，数字人批量口播，AI一键生成画面，配音也不需要真人。大量流水线内容涌入流量池，AI伪造实景画面冒充真实记录，数字人伪装成真人博主带货，AI换脸编造故事。消费者凭肉眼越来越难分辨镜头里是实拍还是算法生成，认真实拍的商家拼不过低成本AI套路铺出来的量。

规则从"建议"变"强制"，意味着平台正式表态：伪装成真人原创的AI内容，不再被默许。

不只是视频号：所有平台都在收网

视频号这刀不是单独砍下来的。把时间线拉出来看，一张越来越密的网正在收紧：2月1日《直播电商监督管理办法》施行，首次明确AI生成内容显著标识的法定要求；7月15日《人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法》实施，抖音、快手、B站同步收紧数字人直播审核；8月7日小红书升级AI治理规则，要求AI虚拟人账号在资料页如实说明创作方式，未标注的AI内容直接限流——截至5月，小红书已累计处置18万篇涉嫌AI造假的笔记，治理超120万个AI托管账号。

海外同样在收网：YouTube去年更新政策打击低质AI内容，Spotify清理了约7500万首违规AI生成音乐。从直播电商到短视频带货，从笔记到音乐，AI内容的"伪装时代"正在全球范围内翻篇。

很多商家担心标注"AI生成"会降低流量。但新规盯的是"伪装成真人原创"的AI内容——合规标注不会直接限流优质AI作品，真正被限制的是靠伪装和欺骗获取流量的流水线劣质内容。规则清晰了，批量蹭流量的低质号会被慢慢清退，真正懂产品、会做内容的创作者反而能拿到更稳定的流量。

对做AI带货的商家来说，最省事的做法是把"AI标注"做成发布流程里的一个固定勾选项，而不是等被限流了再补救。对普通用户来说，以后刷到标注了AI生成的带货视频，至少知道镜头里的画面，可能不是真的。

明天见。

AI-driven commerce sellers now have to "show ID" starting today.

On August 10, a new WeChat Video rule took effect: if a product video uses AI in any form — a digital-human voiceover, an AI-generated product poster, or a synthetic demo of the product in use — it must be explicitly labeled. Miss the label and you face restricted distribution; traffic gets cut directly.

Per Jiemian News's August 3 report, the platform's "AI Application Rules for Short-Video Commerce" upgraded AI labeling from "recommended" to mandatory, listing four situations that must be labeled: AI-generated product display and usage footage, AI human presenters and digital-human appearances, AI-generated product posters and promotional images, and any other AI-generated video footage.

This time, the platform did something platforms hadn't done before: it split commerce scenarios from regular scenarios.

Regular Content Is Voluntary. Commerce Content Must Show ID

The rule's most elegant design is the split. AI creation in regular content is encouraged to be labeled — unlabeled, it still gets traffic. But once a video carries a product link, AI labeling shifts from moral obligation to entry requirement. No label: throttled. Labeled but mismatched with the actual product: also throttled.

The cut is precise. Over the past year, AI video generation got absurdly cheap: a few minutes per clip, mass digital-human voiceovers, AI-generated visuals, no human voice talent needed. Assembly-line content flooded the feed — AI faking real scenes to pose as genuine footage, digital humans impersonating real creators, deepfake narratives. Consumers increasingly can't tell whether a shot is real or synthetic, and honest creators can't outcompete the volume of low-cost AI trickery.

Moving the rule from "recommended" to "mandatory" is the platform's formal statement: AI content disguised as original human work is no longer tolerated.

Not Just WeChat Video: Every Platform Is Closing In

This cut didn't happen in isolation. Pull the timeline out and a tightening net comes into focus: on February 1, the Measures for Livestream E-commerce Supervision took effect, first establishing the legal requirement for conspicuous AI-content labeling; on July 15, the Interim Measures on AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services landed, and Douyin, Kuaishou, and Bilibili tightened digital-human livestream reviews in tandem; on August 7, Xiaohongshu upgraded its AI governance rules, requiring AI virtual accounts to truthfully state their creation method in their profiles, with unlabeled AI content throttled — by May, Xiaohongshu had already processed over 180,000 AI-fraud notes and governed more than 1.2 million AI-managed accounts.

Overseas, the net is closing too: YouTube updated its policy against low-quality AI content last year, and Spotify purged roughly 75 million AI-generated tracks in violation. From livestream commerce to short-video selling, from notes to music, the "disguise era" of AI content is being turned over globally.

Many sellers fear that labeling "AI-generated" will hurt traffic. But the rule targets AI content disguised as original human work — compliant labeling won't throttle quality AI works; what gets hit is assembly-line junk riding on deception.With clear rules, low-quality account farms get flushed out gradually, and creators who actually understand products and content will win steadier traffic.

For AI-commerce sellers, the cheapest move is to make "AI label" a fixed checkbox in the publishing flow, rather than scrambling after the throttle. For ordinary users, at least when a labeled AI-generated commerce video shows up, you'll know the footage might not be real.
