AI的下一块拼图，是操作物理设备的权限

Anthropic Opens the Model Hardware Standard
The Next Piece Is Permission to Touch the Physical World

一份让AI agent安全操作显微镜、移液器和机械臂的共享规范。软件agent的协议战争刚分出格局，物理世界的接口标准已经开始抢占。

A shared specification letting AI agents safely drive microscopes, liquid handlers and robotic arms. Just as the software agent protocol war settles, the physical-world interface race begins.

No.045 2026.08.28 约 4 分钟阅读 ~2 min read

过去两年，AI agent 学会了写代码、订机票、发邮件，甚至能自主完成多步软件操作，但它们始终隔着一层玻璃——看得见显微镜，摸不到旋钮。8月27日，Anthropic 发布 Model Hardware Standard（MHS）研究预览，试图把这层玻璃换成一扇有规矩的门。

让AI“动手”之前，先写好安全手册

MHS 本质上是一份共享规范：定义 AI agent 如何以受控、可审计的方式操作真实物理设备。首批开放对象是科研实验室与先进制造商——在这个场景里，显微镜、移液器、机械臂可以并行接受调度，模型不再是“给建议的顾问”，而是“能上手的研究员”。据财联社报道，采用统一规范后，硬件集成时间有望从数周缩短到数小时，实验室甚至可以让 AI 跑通24小时不间断的自主实验。对日夜排队等机时的科研人员来说，这才是真正的效率跃迁。

与MCP互补，而不是重复造轮子

Anthropic 此前推出的 MCP 解决了模型连接软件世界的协议问题；MHS 瞄准的是另一半——物理世界。两者逻辑一致：先定接口，再定权限，最后才谈能力。当 agent 的操作对象从数据库变成培养皿，“权限”与“安全”的分量完全不同：一次误操作不再是脏数据，而是碎掉的样本和烧掉的电机。这也是 MHS 选择“规范先行、产品在后”的原因——先在纸面上把边界画清楚，再让设备真正动起来。

研究预览，别急着当成标准

需要说明的是，这仍是 research preview，不是已成事实的行业标准。它能否成立，取决于多少设备厂商愿意接入、多少实验室愿意把仪器交出来——协议的价值从来在于被采用，而非被发布。但方向本身值得盯住：当 AI 的下一步是“动手”，谁来定义“怎么动手才安全”，谁就握住了物理自动化的入场券。

数字世界的协议之战已经打完，物理世界的刚刚开哨。明天见。

Over the past two years, AI agents have learned to write code, book flights and send emails — even to orchestrate multi-step software tasks on their own. Yet they have always been separated from the physical world by a sheet of glass: they can see the microscope, but never touch its knobs. On August 27, Anthropic released a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), an attempt to turn that glass into a door with rules.

A safety manual before AI gets hands

At its core, MHS is a shared specification that defines how AI agents may operate real physical devices in a controlled, auditable way. The first adopters are research labs and advanced manufacturers, where microscopes, liquid handlers and robotic arms can be orchestrated in parallel — the model stops being "an advisor with suggestions" and becomes "a researcher with hands." According to Chinese outlet Cailianshe, a unified spec is expected to cut hardware integration from weeks to hours, letting labs run autonomous experiments around the clock. For researchers queuing for instrument time, that is a genuine leap.

Complementary to MCP, not a reinvention

Anthropic's earlier MCP solved the protocol problem of connecting models to the software world; MHS aims at the other half — the physical world. The logic is identical: define the interface first, then permissions, and only then talk about capability. When the object of an agent's action changes from a database to a petri dish, the weight of "permission" and "safety" changes entirely: a bad operation is no longer dirty data, but broken samples and burnt-out motors. That is why MHS ships as a specification first — draw the boundaries on paper before letting devices actually move.

A research preview, not a standard

To be clear, this is still a research preview, not an established industry standard. Whether it holds depends on how many device makers choose to integrate and how many labs hand over their instruments — a protocol's value lies in adoption, not announcement. But the direction is worth watching: when AI's next step is "acting in the real world," whoever defines "how to act safely" holds the ticket to physical automation.

The protocol war in the digital world is over; the one in the physical world has just begun. See you tomorrow.
