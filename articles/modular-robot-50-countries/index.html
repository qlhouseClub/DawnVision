本末科技模块化机器人 3秒合体变形，产品销往全球50国

Benmo Tech's Modular Robot 3-Second Morphing, Sold to 50 Countries Worldwide

当行业热捧机器人「大脑」时，本末科技从本体切入，用一体化直驱关节撬动市场。D1机器人3秒合体变形，轮足整机已出口全球50多国。

While the industry obsesses over robot "brains," Benmo Tech goes after the body with integrated direct-drive joints. Its D1 robot morphs in 3 seconds, and wheel-legged robots have been exported to over 50 countries.

两台双轮足机器人匹配、对齐、合体，然后拐弯、站立——这不是科幻特效，而是中国机器人公司本末科技最新产品D1的真实演示。当人形机器人还在舞台上表演时，这些轮足机器人已经在库房搬水桶、在写字楼送文件。

模块化合体：「反人形」的技术哲学

D1是全球首款整机模块化具身智能机器人，最大亮点是3秒合体变形。双轮足、四轮足、电足、扁片足……多种形态可根据任务自由切换。

酷炫设计背后是扎实的场景逻辑。轻量级扫图任务可以分身成两台并行工作，效率翻倍；遇到楼梯或台阶时再合体，保障机动性。对开发者而言，双轮足与四轮足形态共用一套算法体系，切换SDK即可调整运控需求，调试时间大幅压缩。实地测试也无需同时采购两台机器人，硬件成本直接减半。

除D1外，本末还推出两款成熟整机：刑天——全球首款商业化直驱双轮足机器人，高负载与拉力达80公斤以上，能跳跃、爬坡、半坡驻停，专为库房与园区重体力运输设计；TITA——8个自由度、10公斤动态载荷，零下10度至45度连续作业，支持巡检、物流、数据采集与测绘。

"大脑再好，小脑再灵，身体进不去，一切归零。一个只有60分的身体，连进考场的资格都没有。"—— — 本末科技技术哲学

直驱关节：从零部件到整机的护城河

本末科技的核心竞争力藏在关节里。2020年成立时，公司选择了反主流路线：主打一体化直驱关节，不做减速器。

传统电机高转速、低扭矩，必须通过减速器降速增扭。但减速器带来额外损耗、噪音和寿命折损。工业场景尚可容忍，但若进入家庭，一台整天叮咣作响、几年就坏的扫地机器人根本卖不出去。

拆掉减速器后，本末的关节体积更小、运行更安静，寿命可达5万到10万小时。这一技术选择迅速打开市场：2025年直驱动力模组出货约850万套，在中国消费机器人直驱模组市场拿下61.1%的份额。

从零部件供应商走向整机制造商，是本末的第二步战略。动力模组输出硬件、算法和制造工艺，支撑整机研发；整机进入真实场景，采集稀缺工况数据，反哺关节迭代。两条业务线形成正向循环。

身体入场券：具身智能不能只有大脑

2026年上半年，国内具身智能赛道融资约440亿元，超过一半涌向「大脑派」。然而大量demo只能在工厂等标准场景中运行，遇到门槛、斜坡就歇菜。

行业流行「大脑负责想、小脑负责动」的双层分工，却常常忘了第三层：身体，负责「到」。台阶、碎石路、几十公斤负载，都是实实在在的物理约束，不是光靠训练模型就能解决的。

市场已经给出了答案。本末科技的轮足整机已出口全球50多个国家和地区，是全球轮足机器人出货量最高的厂商。具身的终局或许要靠大脑，但通往未来的入场券，是本体。

明天见。

Two wheel-legged robots align, connect, merge into one — then turn a corner and stand up. This isn't sci-fi movie effects, but a real demo of D1, the latest product from Chinese robotics company Benmo Tech. While humanoid robots still perform martial arts on stage, these wheel-legged machines are already hauling water buckets in warehouses and delivering documents in office buildings.

Modular Morphing: An "Anti-Humanoid" Tech Philosophy

D1 is the world's first fully modular embodied AI robot. Its signature feature is morphing in 3 seconds — dual wheel-leg, four wheel-leg, electric foot, flat foot — multiple forms switch freely depending on the task.

Beneath this eye-catching design lies solid scenario logic. Lightweight mapping tasks can split into two units working in parallel, doubling efficiency; when stairs or steps appear, they merge back together to ensure mobility. For developers, both dual and four wheel-leg configurations share one algorithm system — switching SDKs adjusts motion control requirements, dramatically compressing debugging time. Field testing no longer requires buying two separate robots; hardware costs are cut in half.

Besides D1, Benmo Tech has launched two mature products: Xingtian — the world's first commercial direct-drive wheel-legged robot, with over 80kg crawling load and pulling capacity, capable of jumping, climbing slopes and halting on inclines, designed for heavy-duty transport in warehouses and industrial parks; and TITA — with 8 degrees of freedom and 10kg dynamic payload, operating continuously from -10°C to 45°C, equipped with optical sensors, speakers and cameras for inspection, logistics, data collection and mapping.

"However brilliant the brain, however agile the cerebellum — if the body can't get in, everything amounts to zero. A body that scores only 60 out of 100 doesn't even qualify to enter the exam room."—— — Benmo Tech's technical philosophy

Direct-Drive Joints: The Moat from Components to Whole Machines

Benmo Tech's core competitiveness is hidden in its joints. Founded in 2020, the company took a counter-mainstream approach: focusing on integrated direct-drive joints, no reducers.

Traditional motors have high speed and low torque, requiring reducers to lower speed and amplify torque — just like a fuel car needs a transmission. But reducers add extra loss, noise and lifespan degradation. These issues are tolerable in industrial settings, but for a home robot, a vacuum cleaner that clatters all day and breaks in a few years simply won't sell.

By removing the reducer, Benmo's joints become smaller, quieter, and last 50,000 to 100,000 hours. This technical choice laid the company's hardware foundation and quickly opened up the market: in 2025, approximately 8.5 million direct-drive power modules were shipped, capturing a 61.1% share of China's consumer robot direct-drive module market.

Moving from component supplier to whole-machine maker is Benmo's second strategic step. Power modules deliver hardware, algorithms and manufacturing know-how to support machine development; machines enter real scenarios and collect scarce operational data, feeding back into joint and module iteration. The two business lines form a positive loop.

The Body as Entry Ticket: Embodied AI Can't Be Just Brains

In the first half of 2026, China's embodied AI sector raised about 44 billion yuan, with over half flowing to the "brain camp" — VLA, world models, million-hour datasets, the whole industry charging forward. Yet many demos only work in factory-standard optimized environments; they stall at door thresholds and slopes.

The industry popularizes a two-layer division — "brain thinks, cerebellum moves" — but often forgets the third layer: the body, responsible for "getting there." Steps, gravel roads, tens of kilograms of load — these are real physical constraints that can't be solved by training models alone.

Zhang Di, founder of Benmo Tech, puts it this way: whoever wins in the future — VLA, world models or native embodied models — the final stop for any model will always be deployment on a reliable body. And someone has to build that body now.

The market has already spoken. Benmo Tech's wheel-legged robots have been exported to over 50 countries and regions, making it the world's highest-volume wheel-legged robot maker. The endgame of embodied intelligence may depend on the brain, but the ticket to the future is the body.
